WHAT IS NOW IN THE REPRESENTATION

Two new rows, general, and deliberately two rather than one:

  - the CRITERION that satisfies a value modifier's condition — a qualitative term, exactly as the primary
    event's own condition parameter already was;
  - the TRIGGER that ENDS the modification once satisfied, with no default. An absent value means the
    modification does not persist beyond the event it is evaluated at — never that it persists for ever,
    which is the reading that must not be available by omission.

I kept them separate on your instruction and I want to say why it was the right instruction rather than a
preference. The criterion names a kind of occurrence that nothing can check — whether a touch happened and
possession was kept is a judgement made in play. The termination names a registered trigger, which the
engine CAN check. One field would have had to be typed as the weaker of the two, and that would have thrown
away the only enforcement available on the persistence half. That enforcement turned out to matter within
the hour: see the three goals below.

Neither row is specific to Wide Zone. The criterion row had already been identified independently, months
before this, as the missing home for a GF4 condition. Any modifier can carry either.

Your condition is authored into canonical knowledge as you settled it, with "controlled" as the observable
relationship rather than the word: a touch within the channel after which the touching player's team retains
possession. The persistence is a possession change. Both derive and both reach the resolved game.

And the blocker from 2 October is gone without anyone ruling on it. That analysis named two routes to

THE EXCLUSION, AND WHY THE REGRESSION CASE HAS THREE CASES

Before the repair, naming a forbidden wide channel and naming a permitted scoring line produced the same
verdict: SATISFIED, with the reason "the line it reaches is entailed". The exclusion's own forbidden value
was never compared with anything, because an exclusion cannot entail a line and the generic path reports
satisfaction as soon as a line it touches resolves. An exclusion that reports a compliance it never checked
is worse than an absent one, because the report gets read as evidence.

The repair is general: an exclusion carrying a value requirement is a forbidden-value claim, compared
against the lines on its row within its scope. Nothing is read out of prose, so the forbidden value has to
be stated in typed form — the same treatment your SD-86 already required for a forbidden cardinality, and I
used the same route to supply it. Where it is not typed the item is reported as not evaluable and stays
there, which is the honest state rather than a silent pass.

Three cases, three verdicts: forbidden channel named → UNMET, naming what it forbids and the line that
breached it; permitted line named → SATISFIED; nothing in scope → not evaluable. The test asserts the first
two differ, because a two-case test would have passed against the defect.

WHY QUESTION 3 IS NOT A YES

Two things, both measured, both about the incentive rather than the number — which is why magnitude is not
the lever for either.

THE FIRST: THE CHANNELS REACH THE SCORING LINE. The channels are authored as the full axis extent, end line
to end line, and the scoring line sits on the end line across the full width. So the channels contain part
of the scoring line itself — 15 m of 30 m at the widest permitted extent, 12 m at the preferred 6 m, 9 m at
the authored minimum. Between 30% and 50% of your target line is inside a channel.

A line crossing is defined as a player dribbling the ball over the line or receiving and controlling it
beyond the line. Dribble over the wide part of the line and the last touches are inside the channel, by the
attacking team, which plainly has the ball. So the qualifying condition is satisfied BY THE ACT OF SCORING,
on a third to a half of the line, with no wide play at any point beforehand.

That is a Proportionate failure in your own terms, and no magnitude repairs it: at any multiplier, finishing
wide is simply worth more than finishing centrally, and the incentive stops being about exploiting width at
all. It is also not a rendering or engine problem. It is the channels' authored along-extent meeting the
objective's authored position, and the two were authored by different objects that never had to agree.

THE SECOND: THE GAME SAYS NOTHING ABOUT WHAT HAPPENS AFTER A SCORE. There is no post-score transition, no
restart and no post-score possession procedure — the rejected default left that to realization and nothing
authored it. That did not matter while the modifier applied to a single event. Now that the modification
lasts a possession, it decides whether a second score in the same possession also doubles, and the game has
no answer. The reward per qualifying touch is therefore bounded in time and unbounded in value.

Neither is a reason not to go on the field. You will settle both in the first five minutes of running it,
the way a coach settles anything a rule sheet leaves open. But I am not going to tell you the incentive is
objectively executable when a third of the scoring line is inside the thing that doubles it.
