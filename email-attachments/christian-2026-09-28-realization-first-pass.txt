Subject: The realization layer is built — and no game is eligible for it yet

Christian,

The realization layer exists and does what you specified. The honest headline is
that nothing can currently go through it, and the reason is authoring rather
than engine work. Detail below.

Coach-facing generation remains frozen. Everything here is internal realization
against resolved-game outputs.


1. THE LAYER

Resolved game -> concrete game + realization record. No coach language, no
variation, no activity slots.

It may do exactly three things: choose a value for a line the resolved game
lists as open, inside the bounds carried with it, recording the authority
behind it; instantiate a member satisfying an existential claim; or refuse,
naming every reason rather than the first. It may not fill an unestablished
line, choose outside bounds, add structure, alter a derived value, or proceed
when the game is not eligible.

I built the nothing-closed-without-authority check first, as you asked. All
three acceptance conditions are in, and they read the FINISHED game rather than
trusting the layer that wrote it — so a gap filled by any route, including a
future bug of mine, is caught rather than trusted away. Twenty-two tests cover
it; the suite is 216 and green.

One decision inside it you should know about. A qualitative bound cannot be
checked — SD-15 forbids inventing a number for "a long kick", so there is
nothing to compare a value against. The layer neither rejects the value nor
pretends to have verified it: the choice is recorded as unverified, and the
record says which ones. I would rather it say "I could not check this" than
imply it did.


2. NO RESOLVED GAME IS ELIGIBLE

All five bounded-pilot goals fail Gate A. So does the corpus. So does every
single contract on its own, and every pair of contracts — I searched both
exhaustively rather than sampling.

The blocking check is uniformly GA-NO-FAILED-LINE. Of the 57 unestablished
lines on the corpus, 54 are NOT_AUTHORED and 3 are internal. Broken down by
what the knowledge actually declared on the row:

    43   an object says it needs this row and cannot author it
     8   an object explicitly excludes it
     4   an object looked and declared it does not constrain the row
     2   no declaration reaches the row at all

So the distance to a first realizable game is authoring. Even if a declared
exclusion and a declared non-claim were both ruled not to be failures, 45 lines
would remain, and 43 of those are rows knowledge objects themselves say they
need and cannot supply.

I want to be careful not to overclaim the other direction: 3 of the 57 are
internal and they are mine.


3. TWO THINGS THE LAYER FOUND THE MOMENT IT CONSUMED THE RESOLVED GAME

Both were invisible until something actually read the object.

First: elements with nothing derived were being dropped from the game
altogether. Entries were only ever created while placing a derived value, so an
element whose every line was open or failed appeared in the open and
not-established lists and NOWHERE in the game itself. On the corpus that
dropped two elements the knowledge individuates — an object whose kind is an
open choice between goal and target, and the region the Variable Target
condition establishes. The game was not saying "nothing about this region is
established". It was saying there is no such region.

That is fixed, and two counts now make a recurrence visible rather than silent.

Second: AM-23 gives three reason codes and the declaration vocabulary has five.
NON_CLAIMED has no code and falls through to "coverage"; EXCLUDED is tested
after UNDECLARED, so a silence outranks an explicit exclusion. Eleven of the 57
lines therefore report "nobody looked" when an object did look and declared
either that it does not constrain the row or that it excludes it.

I have not changed the codes or the precedence. Both are semantic and both are
yours. What I fixed is the loss: the declarations reaching a row now travel with
the line, so a reason code can never again be the only surviving account of what
the knowledge said. You can rule on it with the evidence in front of you.

5. RETURNED FOR RULING, NOT RESOLVED BY INTERPRETATION

(a) FIRST_FORWARD_PASS. Minimal proposal: one member added to the trigger
vocabulary, taking it from eight to nine. Nothing else changes. The boundary,
stated so it cannot quietly widen: it is an event in play, like
POSSESSION_CHANGE, not a restart; "forward" is relative to the passing team's
attacking direction, which the representation now establishes, and where
direction is free the trigger is not evaluable rather than failed; "first" is
per episode, using the episode boundary transitions already carry; and it says
nothing about pass success, receiver, distance or intent.

(b) The compound member, REGION_ENTRY {attacking half} + first receiver. You
told me not to create a compound trigger to hide the missing qualifier
capability. Applying that one level further is why I am NOT proposing the
obvious refinement on its own.

The structural half is genuinely small: information-rule triggers have no
qualifier rows, transitions already do, and giving the former the shape the
latter has is mechanical. But it would not fix this member. "First receiver"
names a performer role, and the representation has no performer-role
vocabulary — team roles are explicitly free. Even with the qualifier slot in
place, this member still could not be represented.

So adding the slot would look like progress while leaving the member exactly as
unrepresentable as it is today. That is the same concealment the compound
trigger would have performed, one layer down, and I would rather not do it.
My recommendation is to rule first on whether a performer-role vocabulary is in
scope at all. If it is not, this member should be withdrawn from the permitted
set rather than carried as an alternative nothing can realize.

(c) A third Variable Target edge. VARTARGET-08.a individuates an element class
for an objective-set assignment rule and produces zero lines, because that row
has no field of its own — its only child field is recorded as owned by a
different row. So the assignment rule exists as a class and can carry no value.
Whether the ownership is wrong is a register question I have not answered.

(d) The neutrals count is open and carries two bounds: ">= 1 (no authored
maximum)" and "1-2 (one or two)". Both state numbers, and both are typed
qualitative — so the layer records any chosen value as unverified rather than
checking it against a range it can plainly read. Whether these should be count
bounds, and whether their intersection is the operative one, is a ruling rather
than a parse.

COACH_CUE is held, as you instructed. The open-text subject issue is recorded
and untouched.


6. AFFORDANCE LENSES

My recommendation is to keep them outside the Game Representation. The fields
carry no structural claim — they are about what a player perceives — and
contracting them would have the deterministic representation manufacturing an
affordance, which is the thing you said you did not want.

The measurements, so this is not just a preference. Zeroing all three lens
weights never changes the selected game form — nought out of thirteen — and
changes the constraints in seven of thirteen. And 19 of 39 selected lenses are
realized by no selected constraint at all; only two goals are fully realized,
and two have none.

Rather than a contract, I would propose an invariant: a selected lens must be
realized by at least one selected constraint, or it is not selected. It fails
on 19 of 39 today, which is the honest measure of how far the lens layer is from
meaning anything causally.
