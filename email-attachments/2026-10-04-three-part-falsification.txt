1 · QUALIFIERS OUTSIDE TRANSITIONS — settled, and in your favour

**Qualifier semantics are trigger-owned, not transition-owned, and qualification outside transitions already
exists in running code.** This is the question you most wanted answered and it is settled by precedent rather
than by argument.

The register separates the two itself. The transitions collection declares its selector attributes as
`trigger`, `trigger.region`, `trigger.window`, `qualifier.lastTouch`, `qualifier.endLine`, `qualifier.region` —
**`trigger.region` is part of the trigger; `qualifier.region` is a transition's own field.** And the row's value
type reads "keyed by trigger (… `REGION_ENTRY {region}` …) **plus qualifiers**". The braces are the trigger. The
qualifiers are additional.

Three more pieces of evidence, each independently sufficient:

- `triggerQualifiers` is **keyed by trigger**, and one of its two entries names no row at all — there is no
  fourth qualifier row for time windows. A transitions-scoped block could not be keyed that way.
- You wrote *"no qualifier capability is implied"* **on a trigger**, which only makes sense if qualifier
  capability is a property a trigger can have.
- And decisively, measured in code: the engine builds its trigger set game-wide as `REGION_ENTRY{<region class>}`
  **from the regions**, and its only consumer is a **consequence**. A region-qualified trigger survives to a
  resolved line and the gate validates the argument. That is qualification outside transitions, today, end to
  end.

So the carrier for qualification is **the parameterised trigger value itself** — `NAME {argument}` — which the
engine already parses. **No new field is needed anywhere**, and adding a referent field to consequences or
information rules would introduce a second mechanism for a concept the trigger notation already owns, which the
ownership rule forbids.

One honest caveat: `triggerQualifiers` is read by **no engine code** — it is a prose gloss. So nothing is being
generalised, because nothing consumed it in the first place.

And one limit I will not generalise past: the applicability rule you adopted on 29 September really is
transitions-local — it governs when a transition's own qualifier rows are enumerated. It is not where ownership
lives, and it does not claim to be.

- **No item in any of the eight contracts names a touch or a contact as an event.** I checked every item whose

And the ruled condition itself has never been written down: the criteria document says *"Evaluated, not

**The grain is also wrong, and this is the part I want you to see.** The settled condition is *"a touch within

contact from implying possession or possession change, and forbids an event-history system. So a generic contact
token cannot carry the condition even in principle. Measured: adding a contact token to the vocabulary changed
nothing whatsoever on A04.

**What IS independently supported is a completed pass, on the information rule's trigger row** — the failed line

because Wide Zone wants it.

**And `FIRST_FORWARD_PASS` is not a specialization of it.** Its own semantics say it implies *nothing* about
pass success. A forward pass that does not imply completion is not a special case of a completed pass, so the
vocabulary is **not a hierarchy** — they are independent kinds. Which means your instruction not to assume one
token serves all three was right, and the conclusion is stronger: two of the three are not supported at all.


3 · THE LATEST-CONTACT RELATION — breaks, and the minimum is zero

This is the part I had proposed and it does not hold up.

**The break you asked for.** One relation cannot serve the three consumers, because they do not want three
readings of one state — they want three **different qualification predicates** over contacts:

- the out-of-play qualifier wants the last contact **before the ball left the field**;
- Wide Zone wants the team **performing the qualifying action, at the occurrence**;
- your pass-count ruling wants the last **completed pass**.

A relation whose update rule varies by reader is not a state at all — it is a query over a history, which is
exactly the event-history system you forbade. A relation with one update rule answers at most one of the three
and answers the other two wrongly **without saying so**.

**And the sharper answer is that the one-versus-two question never arises: the minimum is zero.** Nothing
authored entails any member of the last-touch family anywhere in the corpus — the qualifier row carries zero
items against twenty declarations of its absence — and the one slot the corpus does use is structurally closed
to it, because the recency is already carried by the slot rather than by the designation.

So I am not proposing the relation. If you want the capability later, what must be authored first is: what
counts as a contact; its update semantics stated **consumer-independently**, since a per-consumer rule is a
history; and whether it may carry a team qualifier, which the `FIRST_FORWARD_PASS` note refused in advance for
trigger additions.


4 · TEAM ATTRIBUTION — your preference holds, and a wording correction you need

**Team attribution does not belong on the trigger.** Your separation is not merely acceptable, it is the pattern

**But your restatement of the condition changed its meaning, and the difference is exactly what this question

after which that team retains possession."* The settled form, approved 2 October, is *"a touch within the

In the settled form the team comes **from the contact**, and possession is only interrogated afterwards. Your

**The gap that remains is not about where the team lives.** It is that nothing in the representation records
that the qualifying occurrence happened — so the step you leave to "a consumer may then relate the two" is the
step Wide Zone cannot take, whichever way the team is supplied.
