Subject: The four bounded findings — and one thing that cuts across two of them

Christian,

Four findings, in your order. Nothing was changed: no code, no knowledge, no fixture. Two of the
four correct something I would otherwise have told you with more confidence than it deserved, and I
have said where.

Before the four, one thing I found while answering items 1 and 2 that I think reframes both.


THE COACH IS ALREADY TOLD BOTH FACTS, WITHOUT AUTHORITY

The rendered activity already says "a team scores 1 point by getting THE BALL across the marked
line". That sentence is a hardcoded English template. Its cited provenance is the scoring event kind,
the point value and the objective's role — not any object. It has been saying "the ball" since long
before anything in the representation established a ball, and nothing caught that, because the
fidelity check validates numbers in prose and never nouns.

The same is true of possession. The rendered activity says "Play continues when possession changes —
there is no stoppage". That sentence fires on one condition: a transition whose play state is
CONTINUE. It never checks that the transition's trigger is a possession change. I confirmed it
survives changing the trigger to something unrelated, and it survives deleting the possession
relation from the game entirely. The word "possession" occurs exactly once in the whole renderer,
inside that string, and the renderer never reads the possession relation at all. It never reads the
objects list either — zero occurrences of each.

So item 1 is not a choice between telling a coach there is a ball and not telling them. We tell them.
It is a choice about whether what we already tell them has any provenance. I think that is a
different and more uncomfortable question than the one on the table, and you should have it before
ruling on either.


1. BALL EXISTENCE AS DELIBERATE NON-EXPRESSION

WHAT THE EXISTING RULE ACTUALLY REQUIRES. It is narrower than it reads. An excluded property has
three outcomes, not two:

  - if the excluded path happens to be cited by some instruction anyway, the exclusion produces NO
    finding at all. Not a note. Silently dropped — in a file whose own requirement is no silent
    filtering.
  - otherwise it is a VIOLATION if any consequence it names is uncited;
  - otherwise a NOTE.

That is the whole machine-checkable content. The "because" sentence on each entry — the part that
states why the omission is legitimate — is read by no check at any point. It is concatenated into the
note text and nothing else. And an entry that names an EMPTY list of consequences discharges
vacuously, every time, because the filter has nothing to filter. One of the two existing entries
already names an empty list.

YOUR CANDIDATE WORDING IS NOT DECIDABLE BY ANYTHING THE SYSTEM CAN READ. There is no coach model
anywhere, so "cannot change how a competent coach lays out or runs the activity" is not a predicate
the checker can evaluate — it cannot even be approximated. And nothing marks a fact as constitutive:
the Sport Profile file does declare itself as a sport profile, but the loader drops that field, so
the only machine-visible trace is a naming convention in an id string.

As written it is a licence rather than a rule, and I would not have been confident saying so without
testing it, so I tested it. Under your wording, an entry excluding the shared-objective decision —
both teams attack the same line — also discharges, as a passing note. That is the one fact in A04
whose loss the renderer itself warns about, in its own words: a coach "will need it said plainly or
they will set up two targets out of habit". A principle that lets that through is not protecting
anything.

The mechanism makes it worse. Entries match by SUFFIX. Exactly two paths in A04 end in ".kind": the
ball, and the scoring event. A ".kind" entry would silently remove the scoring event from the
load-bearing set. A ".team" entry would remove the shared-objective decision and the possession
holder together. This is not hypothetical; I measured the paths.

WHAT THE BALL ACTUALLY PARTICIPATES IN. Nothing, textually: outside its own entry, no property of the
concrete game contains the ball's id or the word. But it is not inert, and this is where I want to be
precise rather than convenient. The ball is the structural prerequisite that makes the possession-change
trigger constructible at all. It is therefore the precondition of A04's only transition and of the
game's authorization. It does its work one stage upstream of the artifact, which is exactly why a scan
of the artifact finds nothing.

The consequence for your question: the only HONEST entry for the ball is one naming no consequences —
which is the form the rule's own prose says is defensible only for an internal convention rather than
a rule of the game. A ball is not an internal convention. The entry that fits the prose would name the
scoring event as the ball's consequence, which sounds right and is unverifiable: the checker asks only
whether that path is cited, never whether it carries the ball's consequence. One of the two available
entries is dishonest and the other is forbidden by the comment above it.

A correction worth having: there is no separate "ball exists" row. The single status row about the
ball is its KIND, and that row is produced BY the existence item. Excluding the kind is excluding the
existence claim's only trace, not some lesser property of it.

THE SMALLEST PRINCIPLE. If you want one stated, I would put this to you rather than your current
wording — two conjuncts, both computed rather than asserted:

  (i)  nothing established depends on it — no other line's value, selector, referent or geometry
       references this property, derived by the checker rather than declared by an author; AND
  (ii) it was contributed by a constitutive, non-selected source at whole-game scope, so it is
       identical in every activity of this sport and therefore carries no information about THIS one.

Both are measurable. Together they admit the ball and reject the shared-objective decision — which
fails (i), since the target line's own position names it — and reject the possession holder, which
fails (ii).

But I do not think you should rule on that yet, for three reasons:

  - The enabling fix is to the empty-consequences hole, not to the entry list. "There are no
    consequences" should stop being something an author writes and become something the checker
    concludes. That is a code change, not authoring, and it is what separates the ball from the
    shared-objective decision.
  - Given what the renderer already says, the smaller repair may not be an omission rule at all. The
    instruction that needs a ball already names one. Giving that sentence its provenance would carry
    the fact to the coach through the rule that depends on it, with no "use a ball" sentence added,
    and the reported loss would disappear for the right reason rather than by exemption. I have not
    done it; it is a rendering change and you asked for findings first.
  - The ball's whole argument rests on "it participates in nothing", and that holds only while the
    possession relation stays team-only in the concrete game. Either direction of your possession
    ruling moves it. The ball ruling should follow the possession ruling, not precede it.

Not blocked by the ball-count question. But it scopes any eventual entry: it must key on the ball's
kind specifically, never element-wide, because how many balls there are IS a layout fact if anything
ever constrains it.


2. INITIAL POSSESSION HOLDER — I RAN THE COUNTERFACTUAL, AND IT CANNOT FAIL

I held the entire realized A04 constant and swapped only the initially selected holder between the
two teams, then compared every leaf of the concrete game, the entailments, the acceptance account,
the post-realization gates, the rendered activity and the fidelity report.

Five leaves change, and all five are the record of the choice itself: the holder, the choice as
recorded in two places, and the two realization digests — which SHOULD move, because they stamp a
different decision. Everything else is byte-identical, including the full gate object and the full
fidelity report.

AND THAT RESULT IS NOT EVIDENCE FOR WHAT YOU ASKED. I would have reported it as though it were, so I
want to be plain. The test cannot fail on this game. Deleting the possession relation from A04
outright also leaves the rendered activity byte-identical. The reason is the one above: nothing in
the rendering or fidelity path reads possession at all. So the counterfactual measures how the
renderer is wired, not whether the holder means anything.

(There is a second reason, smaller: A04's two teams are interchangeable. A team handle appears in
exactly three places in the whole concrete game — the two team ids and the holder — and the two teams
are identical apart from their ids. Any team-valued property whatsoever would have survived this test,
including a starting-team award, which is unambiguously coach-operational.)

SO THE CLASSIFICATION HAS TO BE ARGUED, AND IT CAN BE. The register's own definition of the relation
is "a team-valued current state", and the possession-change transition is what changes it. Your
boundary ruling says the representation holds the rules governing state, not the state. The initial
value of a state variable is initialization, unless some rule reads it. In A04, nothing does — and
that is a measured property of the game, not of the renderer: A04 has one transition, keyed on the
possession change itself, with no awarded team; its objective is shared by both teams; and the value
modifier accrues to the scoring team. There is no represented relationship that resolves against the
holder.

So yes: in A04 the initial holder is runtime initialization rather than activity-design meaning.

THE BOUNDARY, AND I WOULD STATE IT DIFFERENTLY FROM HOW I PLANNED TO. Not "A04 as resolved" — the
right boundary is "a game in which no established rule reads the possession relation". That is a
narrower claim and a more useful one, because the corpus already contains a game on the other side of
it, derivable today with nothing new authored:

  A01 with the From Goal Kicks situation resolves a restart transition awarded to the DEFENDING team,
  qualified by which end line it went out over (the defending team's) and who touched it last (the
  attacking team); and the Pass Combination Gate counts "ATTACKING_TEAM's connected passes" inside
  "own half of ATTACKING_TEAM". All of those designations resolve against the possession relation.

In that game the initial holder determines, from kick-off, which half the passing count is scored in
and whose passes count. That game derives now; it fails Gate A only on three unauthored restart
placement rows, which is a different matter.

DOES THE FINDING LICENSE NON-EXPRESSION OF THE HOLDER? Not yet, and not for the reason I expected.
The obstacle is not that the holder might matter — in A04 it demonstrably does not. It is that the
thing that would carry it, or decline to carry it, is not wired: no instruction cites possession, and
the one sentence mentioning the word is keyed on something else. Ruling that we deliberately do not
express the holder would be ruling about a mechanism that does not yet read it.


3. WHAT objects[] COUNTS

NOTHING ENTAILS EXACTLY ONE BALL, and the evidence is unanimous rather than merely absent.

Zero items author the object count anywhere in the corpus. Six of the eight restated objects declare
it explicitly unconstrained, four of them naming balls: "doesNotConstrain: number of balls unauthored"
(GF2 — A04's own game form); "objects free (ball kind, count, owner and position reference)" (Wide
Zone — A04's other selected object); "the number of balls is not constrained"; "doesNotConstrain:
number of balls". GF2's own non-claim list puts it in your terms: "That exactly one ball is in play.
GF2 authors no ball count." The strongest authored statement anywhere in the corpus is "none (at least
one ball)" — a floor.

I checked every candidate for an entailment and rejected each: the class cardinality is a minimum of
one with no maximum, and in A04 that value is inert; the possession relation's singularity is a fact
about where the relation lives, not about objects; the Sport Profile's "the ball of SPORT-SOCCER-01"
is a definite article inside a qualitative term, and prose creates no structure; the trigger
construction tests only that at least one ball class exists. Two places in our own files DO assert
one ball, and both are exactly the inference you forbade: a Gate A reading procedure and a ledger
pattern, both heuristics for reading sixty prose activities, neither representation knowledge.

ON YOUR ACTUAL SEMANTIC QUESTION — AND HERE I HAVE TO CORRECT MYSELF. I was going to tell you the
evidence settles on the functional reading. It does not, and the error is a quantifier.

The specification's claim about objects is "every object referenced by a rule, transition or objective
exists". That is a floor in one direction — nothing a rule names may be missing. The converse, that
only what rules reference is in there, is nowhere stated. And the corpus authors the opposite: the
Variable Target Condition requires that "every candidate target object is present in objects[]", on
the authored evidence "All targets stay physically available so the choice remains open; only the LIVE
one scores". Those are objects the live rule does NOT key on, required to be present. The list also
holds goals, lines and gates — laid-out physical equipment, one of them with an authored layout
position.

Your own ratified boundary has both limbs in it: the representation contains what a coach lays out AND
what the rules key on. objects[] today is serving both. What it does not hold is cones, bibs and
markers — and that is because the kind vocabulary has four draft members and nobody has authored more,
not because the structure excludes them.

So the honest answer is that neither of your two readings is established, the question is live, and it
is yours. The reason I am not asking you to settle it now is that it does not need settling for the
count: the count is unestablished under either reading, for reasons that do not depend on which one is
right.

DOES LEAVING IT UNESTABLISHED BLOCK THE GOLDEN CASE? No — measured end to end: Gate A deferred,
realization authorized and accepted, post-realization gates validated, rendering unchanged, the same
two reported losses. The check that would catch a missing required value excuses this one correctly:
the line reads "not constrained", declared non-claimed, and is counted among the established absences
rather than the gaps. That classification is right.

DOES IT WEAKEN IT? Not on the count. The Set up section is regions and dimensions; a coach needs no
number to lay A04 out, and no check and no instruction consumes one. The weakness in this area is at
the kind level — whether a ball is involved at all — and authoring a count would not touch it.

ONE CORRECTION TO SOMETHING I SAID YESTERDAY. I described the ball count arriving in the unestablished
set as the counterpart of possession leaving it. That was right, but I also need to tell you that the
LENGTH of the objects list does not count balls. The register says a count on the collection "adds up"
the per-element counts over matching elements — and no code implements that; nothing reads the
per-element count at all. The list length tracks how many contracts assert a ball. The full corpus has
two ball classes, and our own closure report calls them "the two balls". A04 showing one object means
one contract asserted one, not that there is one ball.

A stale contract comment found in passing, reported not repaired: the types file states that reason
codes are "diagnostic only" and do not change whether a line blocks realization. That is no longer
true — the failed-line check reads the reason code to decide exactly that. The behaviour is right; the
comment above it would mislead anyone reasoning about whether a reason code is load-bearing.


4. THE FIXTURE GUARD

FIRST, THE PROVENANCE BLOCK CANNOT BE THE BASIS OF IT, and I think this is the finding that matters
most in item 4. All four of its fields are weaker than they look:

  - the engine version is a string literal. It has been edited exactly once, when the file was
    created. Everything it claims to version has been rewritten many times over since — derivation
    and realization both, including the clause we added yesterday.
  - the register version is a hand-written sentence. It has not moved across three separate edits to
    the register this week, including two of mine.
  - the derivation-rules version is the literal "rev-5", written out in thirteen places. It names a
    real document, and nothing checks that the two agree.
  - the input digest hashes the input only. With the engine made to return three fewer resolution
    lines — a materially different game — the provenance block came back byte-identical.

One narrowing, because it is nearly true rather than true: eighty-nine of the register's ninety rows
are invisible to the input digest, but the ninetieth is not. The goalkeeper row feeds the session
envelope's role list, and editing it does move the digest. The rule of thumb stands; the absolute
statement does not.

SECOND, DETERMINISM HOLDS. Identical digest across three runs in one process and across separate
processes, through derivation, realization and entailment. There is no clock or randomness in the
path. So a content comparison is sound.

THE PROPOSAL, not implemented:

  One digest taken over the recomputed canonical game, compared against the same digest taken over the
  frozen fixture. If they differ, the suite fails UNLESS a small two-field file records the drift by
  naming the exact live digest and saying why the older game is still the right thing to render
  against.

Four things about it:

  - The acknowledgement lives in its own file, not in the fixture. The fixture's own header says it is
    never hand-edited and the generator rewrites the whole file, so a field inside it would be
    destroyed on every refresh.
  - It never regenerates anything. Refresh stays the deliberate act it is.
  - It cannot be satisfied by doing nothing. With the fixture as it stands and no acknowledgement, it
    fails immediately.
  - The acknowledgement names ONE digest and expires at the next change, so "known stale, forever" is
    not expressible. And if the fixture later becomes current again, an overtaken acknowledgement must
    be deleted rather than left behind to excuse the next drift.

What it costs: the first unrelated change that alters A04's game turns the suite red until someone
either refreshes or writes the acknowledgement with a reason. That is the whole point, and it is the
main cost. It fires on the GAME changing, not on code changing — a refactor or a comment that leaves
the game identical produces nothing. About fifteen seconds added to the suite. One thing to say out
loud rather than discover: the realization choices file comes under the same guard, because
realization is part of the rebuild. That is correct — those choices are part of what defines the
Golden Case — but it should be stated.
