THE CANDIDATE, AND HOW IT BROKE

I proposed putting the qualifying interaction on the value-modifier condition: a closed interaction-kind
vocabulary, named through the existing `condition.type` value `event`, with team and region as qualifiers.
Four things killed it, three of which I had simply wrong.

**1. `condition.type` is not a field I may retype — it is the modifier's identity.** On A04 no item authors it
at all. The value `region` is *derived* from the class's own defining selector, and SD-101 makes that selector
**constitutive of class identity**: an item entailing a different value is preserved and reported as a
contradicted constitutive selector, never resolved against. So retyping it does not reconfigure the modifier,
it contradicts it.

**2. It is a single-valued field.** Exactly five rows carry set multiplicity, and the register's own note says
*"no row gains it whose valueType did not already say so."* So the condition cannot carry a kind **and** a
region. The referent set *can* hold mixed members — I had that forced through and reference integrity still
passed — but a kind token there establishes no identity, and SD-57 forbids promoting open text into event
identity. So the kind has no home on either field.

**3. `event` is not unused, and it is empty for a reason.** GF4 already carries four event-typed modifier
items, and A01-02 authors one too. On the two goals that select GF4, the modifier-overlap invariant is
**already** not evaluable and Gate A **already** fails. The slot is empty of entailments because it is
refused, not because it is free.

**4. Forcing it destroys A04.** Measured, not predicted: retyping the Wide Zone modifier to `event` takes
modifier-overlap from PASS to NOT_EVALUABLE, Gate A to NOT_EVALUABLE, and **realization-authorized from true
to false.** The closure we spent three weeks reaching is gone. And a partial retype is worse — it leaves your
MULTIPLY ×2 reported as a *declared gap*, with two TYPICAL_EXAMPLE items standing in for your own entailment.

**And one silent loss worth recording on its own.** The overlap check routes entirely on `condition.type`.
Retyping takes its region clause from "2 region referents compared" to "0 compared" — and it still reports
**PASS**. Nothing says the comparison stopped happening. That is the project's oldest failure shape, and it
would have been introduced by the repair.

Two further structural refusals, for completeness: there is **no team row anywhere** under the value modifiers
— the six rows are the modifier, its condition type, its referents, magnitude, operation and combination — and
a designation is evaluated *"at the trigger or episode it is attached to"*, which a modifier condition is
neither.


WHAT THE FALSIFICATION SETTLED — and it answers your item 2 directly

**A trigger is not a transition.** The register makes this explicit data rather than a judgement: the rows that
consume the trigger vocabulary are `transitions[]`, `consequences[].trigger`, `informationRules[].trigger` and
`timeWindows[].startsOn`. **Three of the four are not transitions.** So registering a contact as a trigger does
*not* give it play state, episode behaviour or award possibilities — those live on other rows of the
transition, which a trigger carried by an information rule or a consequence never touches.

That is the thing you were right to be wary of, and it turns out to be already solved by the architecture.


AND IT CORRECTED YOUR REQUIREMENT (6)

This is the finding I would most want you to see, because it says your own list asks the carrier to defend the
wrong thing.

**The recency in `LAST_TOUCH` is carried by the slot, not by the designation.** The applicability rule for the
last-touch qualifier reads *"whose touch last played the ball before it left the field."* The **row** means
"last"; the designation only answers *which team*. Which has a sharp consequence:

- filling that slot with `LAST_TOUCH` is a **tautology** — the team that touched last is the team that touched
  last;
- filling it with `ATTACKING_TEAM` is informative — and that is exactly what the corpus does.

So the temporal burden exists whatever designation fills the slot, and it does not arise from the designation.
Asking the carrier for "temporal ordering sufficient for `LAST_TOUCH`" asks it for something the designation
never needed.

What `LAST_TOUCH` **does** need is a **game-level, team-valued latest-value relation** — the team of the most
recent registered contact, with an initial state, a named change event and an explicit unresolved rule. That is
the shape of the possession relation you approved on 2 October, and nothing else in the candidate supplied it.

ITEM 5 — THE TRIGGER-SEMANTICS INTEGRITY CORRECTION

**It is a bounded repair, and most of it already exists.** My earlier report understated this: I said trigger
semantics are never consulted, and the structural half of them is both consulted and already governed by your
own ruling.

The applicability rule you adopted on 29 September reads: *"a transition qualifier applies only where the
transition trigger semantics make that qualifier structurally applicable… Expressed as an applicability rule
over the trigger."* And the governing-line rule already specifies exactly what the `FIRST_FORWARD_PASS` gloss
promises — a dependent line whose governing line is a free choice is **CONDITIONAL**, takes no value, and is not
failed. `CONDITIONAL` is already a registered line state the classifier implements.

So what is missing is narrow: the trigger's evaluability dependency is stated in **prose** in the semantics
block, and the check that admits a trigger tests **list membership only**. The prose can never be the fix —
reading a precondition out of prose is what SD-32 forbids.

**Proposed implementation.** One machine-readable field on a trigger's semantics entry naming the row its
evaluability depends on, and one consultation at the admitting check: a trigger whose named row is not
established yields NOT_EVALUABLE instead of passing. The line status then needs nothing new, because
`CONDITIONAL` and the governing-line rule already do it.

**The limit, so you can judge rather than take my word.** The existing applicability form tests a governing
line's *value membership* against a closed list, and this dependency is about whether a line is **established
at all** — and the direction row's value is "per team, attacks and defends", which is not a list to enumerate.
That is why the dependency cannot simply be an applicability entry, and why I am proposing one field rather
than reusing that form. One field plus one consultation is where I would stop; if you read that as expansion,
say so and I will leave trigger semantics alone until the carrier is settled.

A second, smaller point in the same place: the one site that does read the semantics block only tests that the
block is non-empty, which is true merely because one entry exists. It is not a per-trigger lookup either.
