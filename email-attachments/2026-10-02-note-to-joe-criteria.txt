WHAT I DID
Five questions an authoring decision has to answer before a new incentive meaning goes into canonical
knowledge — his five names: objective, legible, opposition-robust, affordance-preserving, proportionate.
Each one states the question, what passing and failing look like, what evidence answers it, and WHO can
answer it. That last column was the most useful thing to write: some are machine-checkable, some are
coaching judgement, some are only his. Knowing which is which is about half the value.

I kept it to a checklist for a conversation. No tooling, no gate, nothing in the engine reads it. He said he
didn't mean the broader Change Assurance System yet and I took that literally.

I GROUNDED IT RATHER THAN INVENTING IT
Everything traces to material already in the repo — the incentive invariant from
CONSTRAINT_INCENTIVE_FRAMEWORK.md, "invite not force", GF11's compactness-emergent finding, his
scoring-ownership rule, the five influence dimensions. The only new thing is that they're asked as
questions, at authoring time, about a specific decision.

Worth knowing: the guardrail currently exists as prose on library rows and as wording checks in
incentive-expression.ts. Those stop us PHRASING an incentive as an instruction. They say nothing about
whether its meaning is ecologically sound. That's the actual hole.

THE WORKED EXAMPLE IS THE PART THAT EARNS IT
I ran ball / player / touch through all five and did not pick. The criteria separate them cleanly:

- PLAYER fails three, and two failures are against AUTHORED knowledge, not taste — park a player in each
  channel and the condition is permanently satisfied, which contradicts the library row's own audit "Zones
  optional" and the contract's own "Entering must not be compulsory". So it reads as unavailable, not just
  unattractive. That's the strongest kind of finding these criteria can produce.
- BALL is weak on proportionality for a number I checked: the two channels are 50% of A04's realized width
  (7.5 m each on 30 m), so mere ball presence is satisfied incidentally and ×2 makes the base value
  decorative.
- TOUCH is strongest on the three criteria carrying the ecological intent, weakest on the one the engine
  cares about.

So his worry is confirmed rather than theoretical: the easiest reading to evaluate deterministically is not
the one the criteria favour. Had we optimised for convenience we'd have picked ball.

I also found that criterion 4 already has a case waiting — the Round 7.4 question about whether an explicit
attacker-side Transition Bonus over-scripts the race, which the framework itself says shouldn't be settled
as a one-off. Good argument for adopting them.
