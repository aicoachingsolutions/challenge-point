WHY I SAID NO TO QUESTION 3

Two measured things, neither of which magnitude can fix:

  1. The channels are authored "end line to end line", and the scoring line sits on the end line. So the
     channels CONTAIN part of the scoring line — 15 m of 30 m at the widest permitted width, 9 m at the
     authored minimum. A line crossing is defined as dribbling the ball over the line, so dribbling over
     the WIDE part of the line satisfies the qualifying condition in the act of scoring. The incentive is
     collectable by finishing wide, with no wide play at any point. That is a Proportionate failure on his
     own criteria and no multiplier repairs it.

  2. The game says nothing about what happens after a score — no post-score transition, no restart, no
     possession procedure. That did not matter while the modifier applied to one event. Now it lasts a
     possession, so it decides whether a second score in the same possession also doubles.

Both are decisions he would settle in the first five minutes on the pitch. Neither is a blocker for a pilot
he runs himself. But telling him the incentive is "objectively executable" while a third of the scoring line
sits inside the thing that doubles it would have been exactly the kind of green light this whole process
exists to prevent, so I said no and explained why rather than hedging it into a yes.
