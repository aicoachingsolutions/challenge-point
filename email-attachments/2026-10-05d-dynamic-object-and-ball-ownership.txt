1 · THE HAZARD — closed first, and the register had already promised it

Done before the applicability change, as you sequenced it.

**Two things I should correct about my own earlier report.** First, the hazard was **latent, not live**: after
classification there are **zero** surviving conditional lines anywhere in the corpus or any goal — every one
resolves to enumerated or withdrawn. I implied otherwise. Second, and more to the point:

**The register had already promised this protection, in writing, and the implementation dropped it.** From the
applicability block's own grammar note: *"Where the selector does not fix the attribute the condition is
UNDECIDABLE and the line is KEPT, so an applicability rule can never hide a real gap."* The line **was** kept.
And then the assembly step dropped every conditional entry from all three lists, so it was kept and invisible.
**That is the seventh registered-but-unenforced construct this week, and the first where the register itself
stated the guarantee it did not get.**

**It fires on precisely the change you authorized**, which is why your sequencing was right. The selector matcher
returns undecided when a selector *straddles* the applicability list, and GF4's target object is selected
`kind ∈ {goal, target}` — so against a list naming `goal` and not `target` it straddles, goes conditional, and
vanished. Silent lines went 22 to 28 in the corpus.

**The fix.** A conditional line is now reported as not established, with the reason *"applicability unresolved"*,
and withdrawn stays silent — because those are opposite cases: withdrawn means the condition is **false** so
nothing is owed, conditional means it is **undecided** so whether anything is owed is itself undecided. **The
closure half comes for free**, because the acceptance condition that checks nothing was closed without authority
ranges over exactly that list: a concrete game that fills a conditional line is now refused.

**Tested against a forced straddle, not against the corpus.** The corpus produces no conditional line, so a fix
whose only evidence is "nothing changed" would be no evidence at all.


2 · THE APPLICABILITY ENTRY — applied, and your counterfactual verified

Both position rows now carry an entry conditioned on the element's own kind, listing the static kinds. A ball is
absent from that list, so its position rows are withdrawn.

Your three conditions, measured on real authored knowledge — A01 with From Goal Kicks, which authors a ball:
**ball present** in the game as `{kind: "ball"}` with no position key; **no invented static layout position**, the
rows are withdrawn rather than gapped; **no false realization freedom**, nothing on objects is open.

And the rule is about state-dependence rather than about objects: **GF4's static target keeps its layout position
and is still offered as a choice.** A04 is untouched — no ball, so no object and no position question at all.

**I had to add one word to the applicability grammar, and I want that in front of you rather than buried,
because you liked this route specifically for adding no concept.**

My first version wrote the entry as a positive list — the row applies to `goal`, `line`, `gate`, `zone`,
`target`. It was wrong, and an existing test caught it: an object selected `kind=goalA` **lost its authored
layout position**, because anything not on the list is treated as not applying.

The reason is structural rather than a missing entry. This mechanism's own rule, stated in two places in the
code, is that an applicability condition *"may only ever remove a line it can positively disqualify"* — an
undecided condition keeps the line, so a rule can never hide a gap. A positive list satisfies that for a closed
attribute like a trigger. It cannot for `kind`, which is open-ended: two kinds in live corpus selectors (`zone`,
`target`) are already absent from the register's own draft list, and any kind nobody enumerates is silently
disqualified. **A positive list makes "static" the exception and "dynamic" the default, which is the reverse of
what you ruled.**

So the condition now names the ball and nothing else — `notIn: ["ball"]` — which disqualifies exactly the kind
whose location is state-dependent and keeps every other kind, including kinds nobody has named yet. That needed
one alternative key in the grammar's condition form, validated as mutually exclusive with the positive one.
Measured after the change: an unenumerated kind keeps its position, a registered static kind keeps its position,
and only a ball is withdrawn.

It is one key, it is data rather than special-case code, and it makes the mechanism obey its own stated rule for
this attribute. But it is a concept addition and it is reversible, so say the word if you would rather have it
back out and the rule placed elsewhere.

**One smaller thing while we are here.** `zone` and `target` appear in live corpus selectors and not in the
register's draft kind list. Either the list needs extending or those selectors are non-conformant. Not urgent,
and the negative form means nothing now depends on resolving it.

4 · THE OWNERSHIP QUESTION — your hypothesis is right, and the gap is real

**Where does the architecture establish the sport is soccer?** Nowhere, as a fact a derivation can use.

The soccer module does carry a sport identity — it declares itself `sport.soccer`, and every row of the
performance-context registry is tagged with it. But the sport of a *run* is fixed by a **build-time import**, not
by any knowledge layer: there is no runtime sport dispatch anywhere, and the coach's own sport text is
concatenated into a keyword-matching blob. So the sport is a property of which library is compiled in, not a fact
a game is told.

**Does an existing authoritative layer own that the functional object is a ball?** **No — and the option you
would most naturally reach for is closed.** The engine has three support channels: an entailing item, a citable
standing decision, and the session. For a *value* all three work. For **existence** there is exactly one:
elements are formed from contract items and nothing else, and the boundary is named — SD-83, *"an exclusion, an
assumption, engine wording or an out-of-boundary note may constrain what exists; none of them brings it into
existence."* So **a standing decision cannot establish a ball**, however plainly it says one exists; it can only
supply values to elements something else created. The session carries four envelope numbers and nothing else.

As for the sport module: **the derivation and realization cores are genuinely decoupled from it** — verified by
walking the module graph, not by a search for its name. I should be exact rather than sweeping, because my first
draft said there was no path at all and that is wrong: the selection step lives in the derivation directory and
does import the library the sport module docks into. What is true is narrower and still decisive — nothing the
sport module knows ever becomes **authoritative support** for an element. It reaches selection; it does not reach
establishment.

**The decisive evidence is the specification's own Owner column.** §5.4 Objects names the owner as *"SELECTION;
REALIZATION for position within bounds"* — so object existence and kind are owned by selection, with realization
co-owning only position within bounds. **No sport, module or game-level owner appears in section 5's ownership
column at all.** And the spec demonstrably *can* express a second co-owner when it means to: §5.3 gives `teams[]`
the owner *"SELECTION, reconciled against SESSION"*. For Objects it does not.

So, in your terms: **this is the ownership gap, and I am returning it rather than filling it.** The two existing
items that do establish a ball — in the goalkeeper build-out and the goal-kicks situations — are both
object-scoped, neither sport-level. Nothing owns it as an invariant.

**You have already ruled on the adjacent case, and it endorses what is happening now.** KR-04, in your words:
*"RPC-001 does not own or instantiate the physical carrier of its scoring event ... The resolved game must
independently contain a supported compatible carrier — line, zone, gates, target player — and the gates must
establish that the chosen event can operate through it. If selection produces an RPC-compatible scoring event for
which no supported physical carrier exists, reconciliation fails/returns to selection. RPC-001 does not
manufacture the carrier conditionally."*

That is this situation exactly, one carrier over. It says a context must not own the carrier, that the game must
independently contain one, and that when it does not, **the right outcome is a failure that returns to
selection** — which is precisely what A04 now does. Your prior ruling predicted the failure we are looking at,
and it points the answer at selection rather than at a new layer.

**And a precise answer to why A04 in particular has none:** it routes to RPC-004 Chance Creation, **RPC-004 has no
contract in the corpus at all**, and its two contracted objects both declare objects NON_CLAIMED. So A04's gap is
not an oversight in GF2 or Wide Zone — it is a context with no contracted knowledge behind it.

**One thing to flag before it becomes another RC-19.** §5.4's Claim column reads *"Every object referenced by a
rule, transition or objective exists **and is positioned** inside `area`"*. Your ruling today amends that: a
dynamic object exists and is **not** positioned. The spec sentence is now partly superseded, and we have just
spent a day on what happens when a superseded rule stays cited. Worth correcting while it is cheap.

**But your hypothesis needs no new layer to act on.** A knowledge object *is* the existing ownership mechanism,

**One implementation detail to carry into that decision, found while checking the above.** A knowledge object

TWO THINGS I FOUND ON THE WAY, BOTH REPORTED RATHER THAN REPAIRED

**1. The applicability mechanism can only express half your ruling, and the other half needs you.** Your sentence
has two parts: a dynamic object *may* exist without a position, and a position *is* required where authoritative
knowledge establishes one. The entry delivers the first. It cannot deliver the second, because an applicability
withdrawal is absolute — and worse, **an authored value on a withdrawn line is silently discarded.** So a ball
whose starting position your knowledge *did* fix would have that position quietly ignored.

Two things keep this from being urgent. It is **general and pre-existing**, not something the new entry
introduced — an authored qualifier on a transition that withdraws it behaves identically, under an entry that
predates this by a week. And **no corpus knowledge authors a ball position**, so nothing is lost today. But the
second half of your ruling is not implemented, and implementing it needs either a defeasible withdrawal or a
decision that an authored ball position is a knowledge error to be reported rather than honoured. That is yours.

A consequence I did handle: one existing test asserted that a dynamic location used geometrically is refused, and
it used a ball to do it. With the ball's position rows withdrawn, the refusal had nothing to fire on. I moved the
fixture to a gate and **verified the refusal still fires there** — a dynamic location on a *static* object's
layout row is the genuinely pathological case, so the invariant is better tested than before, not weaker.

**2. The guard that keeps the derivation layer sport-neutral has a hole exactly where the real coupling is.** Its
term list covers soccer, football, goalkeeper, dribble, throw-in, corner kick, penalty kick, final third,
midfielder — and **not `ball`**. The single sport-specific literal in that layer is `'ball'`, in the line your
possession-change prerequisite is implemented on. The guard does work: it caught the phrase "in football" in a
doc comment of mine last week. It simply does not know the one word that matters here.

That bears on your ownership question more than it looks. The engine currently recognises a ball by a hardcoded
string in sport-neutral code, which is the strongest practical argument that ball existence belongs in knowledge
rather than in the engine — and the guard would not have told us.
