THE SHORT VERSION

The representation already has a canonical, general identity mechanism that carries no domain meaning:
`elementId`, the class id, minted from the item that authored an element. It is exactly what your
principle describes, and it would apply to teams with no new semantics.

It cannot mint more than one handle per authoring item, because the handle IS the item. An existential
claim of cardinality 2 is one authoring act, so two teams arrive with one name.


1 · WHAT ALREADY EXISTS

Three identity mechanisms, one quantifier, and two things that look like identity and are not.

Identity: the class id `c:<contract>:<item>`; the typed structural reference that resolves to it
(SD-98, already graded HELD / DANGLING / OPEN_TEXT); and the authored selector, which is what tells
A04's two wide channels apart.

Quantification: `scope: PER_TEAM`. This is the existing way to say something about every team, and it
needs no identity. Its limit turns out to be the whole question — it gives quantification, not
co-reference. "Each team" needs no identity. "The other team" does.

Not identity: `satisfies`, which every member of one claim shares; and the index, which is the only
thing that actually discriminates two teams today.

One structural fact that bounds all of it: no register row anywhere names an id, name, label, key or
identity leaf. `performers.teams[]` has exactly four rows — the collection, outfieldCount, goalkeeper,
roles. The register gives a team no identifying property at all, so authored knowledge has nowhere to
put one. Identity lives entirely outside the register, as engine-minted provenance.


2 · IS ANY OF IT GENERAL ENOUGH FOR TEAMS?

Yes in kind, no in cardinality. All three identity mechanisms identify an element by **what authored
it**. None can identify an element that nothing individuates.

So it is general, and teams are the only collection that currently exercises it — because teams are the
only collection whose members are created by a claim with cardinality above one rather than authored one
item at a time. Across A01–A06 the only claim with a shortfall anywhere is the teams claim. A04's
objective claim has shortfall zero; the regions, transition and value modifier are all authored elements
with their own ids.

The regions are the near-miss worth naming, because they look like the case and are not. Two channels,
structurally similar, independently referable — but each has its own authoring item, hence its own id,
plus a distinguishing authored selector. **The corpus has never yet had to refer to one of N
indiscernible instances.** Teams are the first.


3 · DOES EXISTING KNOWLEDGE REQUIRE IT — and the correction I owe you

**The case I went in believing does not require identity.** GF2's P2 — "equal to the other team's
outfieldCount" — reaches for "the other team", but the claim is **symmetric**: it asserts the two counts
are equal. A set-level comparison expresses that exactly, with no identity. And the engine already does
it — the roster entailment reads the authored equality and divides the session total.

So the one selected consumer is already served without identity, which is why nothing has been blocked
until now. I had this the wrong way round in my last note, where I implied P2 was the strongest case.

The requirement splits, and the split is the useful finding:

  - symmetric per-team claims — `scope` plus a set-level comparison. No identity.
  - asymmetric per-team claims, and possession assignment — identity required.

What genuinely requires it:

**GF4 authors an asymmetry.** Its P2 reads "unequal between the teams, e.g. 4 and 6 (4v6)", PER_TEAM,
AUTHORED. A static structural asymmetry with nothing to do with possession — "4 and 6" cannot be stated
unless the representation can say which team has 4. Two qualifications: the status is TYPICAL_EXAMPLE, so
the numbers are an example rather than a bound, though the asymmetry itself is the authored claim either
way; and GF4 is contracted but not selected by any current goal. The roster entailment refuses on it, so
a GF4 game today gets no roster rather than a wrong one.

**The Neutral Player Condition quantifies with a complement** — "for each team T: P2(T) > P2(other team)
− P5". A bound variable and its complement, explicitly, and asymmetric. Contracted, not selected.

**Possession assignment** is the live consumer, and chronologically the third rather than the first.

The pattern underneath: three different authors reached for a complement or an asymmetry, and all three
were recorded SCHEMA LOCAL. The restatements have been reporting this gap for some time without naming
it.


4 · THE MINIMUM DISTINCTION

One criterion, and it is testable: **can swapping it between two members make the representation false?**

If yes it is a value — a claim that can be true or false, needing a row, a basis, a status and support.
`designation` was this, and appears zero times in the register: there was no row to hold it and no line
to carry a verdict.

If no it is an address — it makes no claim and cannot be wrong. It needs only to be unique among its
siblings and stable. The class id is this.

So a handle must be unique among its collection, stable across stages and across two runs, opaque
(equality and complement the only operations — no ordering, no arithmetic, no string matching, no
recovering the instantiation order), unauthored (no row, no line, no verdict, no status, and no item may
target it), and unrendered.

A name, a colour and an attacking/defending designation each fail the first test: swap them and the
representation says something different. Each is a value needing support that does not exist.


5 · AT WHAT STAGE — tested, not assumed

Your suspicion was right, and testing it sharpened it to **instantiation**, inside realization.

It cannot be earlier: before realization the teams are absent from the game and the claim's shortfall is
two, so there is nothing to carry a handle. And it is not needed earlier: no line referring to an
individual team is enumerated before realization. Member lines come from established members, and A04's
only established members are an "objective-area" string and a typed reference to a channel — neither is
a team. No P2 line is enumerated in any of A01–A06.

The refinement worth having tested for: **instantiation rather than final assembly**, even though
assembly is where the first per-team value gets written. The invention check already has to address
members individually during realization, and uses the index to do it. Mint at assembly and the index
stays the operative identity for the whole of realization, so the two notions coexist. Mint at
instantiation and consume at assembly, and there is one.


6 · THE FALSIFICATION CASE

Two structurally identical teams — A04 already is this, both members identical.

(a) Referability: there are handles h1 ≠ h2, each resolving to exactly one member, and for each one "the
other team" resolves to the other member and nothing else. That is the whole of what an asymmetric
per-team claim needs.

(b) Permutation invariance: build the game twice with the handles exchanged. Modulo renaming the
handles, every artifact must be identical — resolved game, realization record, post-realization
verdicts, fidelity verdicts. The rendered coach text must be identical **literally**, not modulo
renaming, because no handle may reach a coach.

A failure of (a) means the mechanism is not identity. A failure of (b) modulo renaming means the handle
carries meaning. A failure of (b) literally in the render means the handle leaked to a coach.

**And the case diagnoses today exactly: A04 fails (a) outright and passes (b) vacuously** — because the
two members are identical objects, so exchanging them is the identity operation. There is nothing to
permute. That is why no check has caught this: the game is currently perfectly symmetric, and the index
is harmless precisely because nothing yet distinguishes the members. The first asymmetric per-team value
breaks the symmetry.

So the test has to be run with a distinguishing value forced in, or it passes without proving anything.


WHERE THE SAME PROBLEM IS HIDDEN — blast radius, not repaired

Can produce a wrong answer:

**A01, A02 and A05 carry two separate claims on the teams collection**, and nothing can see that they
refer to the same teams. A01's are one team (no maximum) and two teams (maximum two). I ran the
authorization: two teams are refused whichever claim they are attributed to, and **three teams draw no
team-related objection at all.** The per-claim maximum counts only that claim's own instantiations, and
the collection-level cardinality check runs before instantiation, so nothing sees the third team. This
is the same error class as the three Wide Zone channels — multiple source rows not entailing multiple
physical elements — and the reason the engine cannot apply your ruling here is exactly the missing
identity. Latent only because A01's Gate A is not evaluable, so it is not realized today.

**Per-team values are addressed by index** at final assembly. Safe today only because the roster
entailment refuses unless equality is authored — so it writes a per-index number only where the index
cannot matter. That guard is an equality requirement, not an identity requirement: the safety is
incidental. GF4 authors unequal teams.

Degrades a check rather than the game:

**DISTINCT_ON degenerates silently.** It keys its tuples by the element's path segment, which is the
shared `satisfies` for two members of one claim — so the second overwrites the first and the
pairwise-distinctness check passes having compared nothing. The one authored DISTINCT_ON applies to three
separately-authored object classes with real ids, so this is latent. It is the sharpest evidence that
identity is general: the check exists to prove two members are in different places, and it is disabled
for exactly the population that has no identity.

**The derivation-layer dedupe drops a twin** — two structurally identical members both key to the same
string, and the first is kept. Latent for teams, same assumption one layer down.

**One reported path collides** for two members of one claim. The accounting underneath uses the index, so
no value is mis-attributed; what collides is the path printed in a failure message. Cosmetic, but it
would name one path for two members to whoever reads it.

**And there are three unrelated spellings of member identity** across the layers, none general — the
signature of a concept that exists and is unowned.

On determinism: member order *is* stable across two runs of one input. Worth saying plainly, because
"it's fine, it's deterministic" is the wrong consolation — the problem is not that the index changes, it
is that nothing the game holds explains it.


THE ONE RULING THIS NEEDS

The mechanism exists; what is missing is a mint for an element that no item individuates. The smallest
general addition is to make the existing handle total — derived from the claim that authorized the
member, plus a discriminator.

**The discriminator is yours, because two of your rulings meet there.** That position is an
implementation artifact and not authored football meaning; and identity may be required without carrying
domain meaning. Those are compatible only if minting is separated from interpreting: an ordinal may be an
*input* to minting an opaque handle, provided nothing afterwards can read it, order by it, or recover it
— which is what the opacity requirement and the permutation test exist to enforce.

So: is an ordinal permissible as an input to minting an opaque handle, with those two as the guards? If
yes, the addition is small, general and testable, and it uses the canonical mechanism rather than
creating a team concept.

Or must the handle's derivation contain no ordinal at all? Then the evidence says identity cannot be
established from the corpus — the only remaining sources are an authored distinguishing property, which
does not exist and which you forbade inventing, or an opaque counter, which is an ordinal wearing a hat.
On that reading **the two teams are genuinely indiscernible and the representation is right to refuse to
tell them apart**, and GF4's asymmetry and possession assignment are blocked on an authoring decision
rather than an engine extension.

Both are coherent. The second is more conservative and may well be the right answer; it would make this
an authoring question rather than a representational one. I have not chosen between them.

Nothing implemented. The touch trigger and the magnitude are untouched, and neither reading affects
