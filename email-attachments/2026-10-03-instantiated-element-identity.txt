You were right to make me check before proposing anything. It is a general instantiated-element reference
question, not a team-specific gap — and checking it turned up two corrections I owe you plus a mechanism

THE SHORT VERSION

The canonical handle already exists and already carries no domain meaning: `elementId`, the class id,
minted from the item that authored an element. It is exactly what your principle describes. **It cannot
mint more than one handle per authoring item, because the handle IS the item** — and an existential claim
of cardinality two is one authoring act, so two teams arrive with one name.

And the requirement kind that would consume a team handle already exists too. More on that below, because
I think it is the most useful thing in this check.


1 · WHAT ALREADY EXISTS, AND WHY NONE OF IT REACHES A TEAM

Three identity mechanisms — the class id; the typed structural reference that resolves to it, already
graded HELD / DANGLING / OPEN_TEXT; and the authored selector, which is what tells A04's two wide channels
apart. One quantifier, `scope: PER_TEAM`. And two things that look like identity and are not: `satisfies`,
which every member of one claim shares, and the index.

> **All three identity mechanisms identify an element by WHAT AUTHORED IT. None can identify an element
> that nothing individuates.**

That is the whole finding, and it is general rather than about teams. Teams are the only collection that
currently exercises it, because teams are the only collection whose members come from a claim with
cardinality above one rather than authored one item at a time. One qualification: corpus-wide there is a
second such claim, on the objective sets, which never reaches a run because the Variable Target Condition
is in no goal's selection — and that case would be harder, because its members list holds references
pointing out of an instantiated member.

The wide channels are the near-miss worth naming, because they look like the case and are not: each has
its own authoring item, hence its own handle, plus a distinguishing authored selector. **The corpus has
never yet had to refer to one of N indiscernible instances.** Teams are the first.

Two structural facts bound all of it. **No register row anywhere names an id, name, label, key or identity
leaf.** A team has exactly four rows — the collection, outfieldCount, goalkeeper, roles. The register gives a
team no identifying property at all, so authored knowledge has nowhere to put one.

And the second closes off the selector route for teams specifically: **the teams collection declares the
selector attribute `team`, and neither of its field rows declares a selector attribute at all.** So `team` is
wholly unbacked, and the relation that turns a selector term into a property value requires a field row
declaring it. **The authored-selector route — the very thing that tells the two wide channels apart — is
structurally unavailable for a team**, independently of the instantiation problem. That matters for the
ruling at the end, and I flag it there.

And the limit `scope: PER_TEAM` runs into is the question in miniature: it gives universal quantification,
not co-reference. **"Each team" needs no identity. "The other team" does.**


2 · THE FIRST CORRECTION — GF2-14.b does not require identity

In my last note I implied it was the strongest case. It is not. "Equal to the other team's outfieldCount"
reaches for an individual, but **the claim is symmetric**: it asserts the two counts are equal, which is
decidable over an unordered pair. The roster already satisfies it without ever resolving "the other team"
— it reads the word "equal" and divides the pool.

So the requirement splits, and the split is the useful part: symmetric per-team claims need no identity;
asymmetric ones do.

**What genuinely requires it is your own caution on ruling C31b**, which is stronger evidence than any
item in the corpus:

    "Please preserve the ability for other selected authoritative knowledge to establish numerical
    asymmetry where the learning problem requires it. Equal team numbers should not become an
    engine-level universal assumption."

and the fitNote on the same ruling says how — a game form authoring asymmetry displaces GF2-14.b through
the ordinary displacement mechanism. **A displacing game form must say which team gets which number.** So
asymmetry is not hypothetical; it is a capability you have already directed be preserved, and it cannot be
expressed without identity.

GF4 is exactly such a game form — its P2 reads "unequal between the teams, e.g. 4 and 6 (4v6)". Two
qualifications: the status is TYPICAL_EXAMPLE, so it bounds and never entails, and GF4 is contracted but
not selected by any current goal. The roster refuses on it, so a GF4 game gets no roster rather than a
wrong one.

Two further shapes are latent and both blocked on authority rather than mechanism: the Pass Combination
Gate's SV1, registered as a view **keyed by team** ("halves and thirds along the axis, per team"), where
possession can pick the key but cannot supply the mapping, because the per-team halves must stay stable
while possession alternates; and the Variable Target Condition's asymmetry, which rests on an ASSUMED item
while the row it needs is simultaneously NOT_AUTHORED.

**So the strict answer: among knowledge that actually entails anything today, possession assignment is the
only consumer.** Identity is a prerequisite, not an unmet existing requirement. That is a weaker claim
than I made last time and I think the right one.

On the vocabulary: `TEAM_<id>` is used three times, and in no case does an authoritative item require a
named team. The clearest is an AUTHORED **exclusion** — no neutral may be fixed to a named team — which
needs the form to be expressible so the prohibition is not vacuous. That is a much weaker demand than any
team having identity, and it is exactly the asymmetry you suspected between a vocabulary contemplating
something and an item requiring it.


3 · THE MECHANISM WE ALREADY HAVE AND HAVE NEVER USED

`COMPARES` is a registered requirement kind. You added it on 20 September under AM-16, with a full form —
left operand, operator, right operand, operators including equality — and operand forms under SD-23: a
represented game property `{row, selector}`, or a deterministically derived quantity over supported
properties. The register even versions it: "contractEnums.requirement: 2 (2026-09-20, COMPARES added by
AM-16)".

**No corpus item uses it.** Zero.

Three consequences:

Four P2 fitNotes carry the note "No requirement kind compares two elements … (SCHEMA LOCAL)". **Those were
true when written and are now stale** — AM-16 added exactly that kind. Four items are declared blocked on
a limitation that no longer exists.

Your own rule points here before anything new: a new mechanism must not be introduced where an existing
canonical one already owns the same semantic concept. COMPARES owns comparison between two represented
properties.

And it localises exactly what identity is for. A symmetric comparison needs two operands ranging over the
pair. **An asymmetric one needs a selector that picks out ONE team** — and a selector that picks one of
two indiscernible members is precisely the missing handle. It would also replace a prose test: the roster
currently reads equality by matching the word "equal" against the item's text, which is how the near-miss
on "unequal between the teams" arose in the first place.

So the shape of the answer is not "mint a handle" on its own. It is: **the comparison kind already exists,
is unused, and is what a team handle would be for.**


4 · THE MINIMUM DISTINCTION

One criterion, and it is testable: **can swapping it between two members make the representation false?**

If yes it is a value — a claim that can be true or false, needing a row, a basis, a status and support.
`designation` was this, and appears zero times in the register: no row to hold it, no line to carry a
verdict. If no it is an address — it makes no claim and cannot be wrong; it needs only to be unique among
its siblings and stable.

So a handle must be unique, stable, **opaque** (equality and complement only — no ordering, arithmetic,
string matching, or recovering the instantiation order), **unauthored** (no row, line, verdict or status,
and no item may target it), and **unrendered**. A name, a colour and an attacking/defending designation
each fail the swap test, so each is a value needing support that does not exist.


5 · AT WHAT STAGE — tested, not assumed

Your suspicion was right, and testing sharpened it to **instantiation**, inside realization.

It cannot be earlier: before realization the teams are absent from the game and the shortfall is two, so
there is nothing to carry a handle. And it is not needed earlier: no line referring to an individual team
is enumerated before realization — member lines come from established members, and A04's only two are an
"objective-area" string and a typed reference to a channel. No P2 line is enumerated in any goal, and the
engine skips field-line enumeration for an existential class outright.

The refinement worth having tested for: **instantiation rather than final assembly**, even though assembly
writes the first per-team value. The invention check already addresses members individually during
realization, using the index. Mint at assembly and the index stays operative for all of realization, so
two notions coexist. Mint at instantiation and consume at assembly, and there is one.


6 · THE FALSIFICATION CASE

Two structurally identical teams — A04 already is this.

(a) Referability: handles h1 and h2 distinct, each resolving to exactly one member, and for each one the
complement resolving to the other member and nothing else. That is the whole of what an asymmetric
per-team claim needs.

(b) Permutation invariance: build the game twice with the handles exchanged. Modulo renaming, every
artifact identical. The rendered coach text identical **literally**, since no handle may reach a coach.

A failure of (a) means the mechanism is not identity. A failure of (b) modulo renaming means the handle
carries meaning. A failure of (b) literally in the render means it leaked to a coach.

**And the case diagnoses today exactly: A04 fails (a) outright and passes (b) vacuously**, because the two
members are identical objects, so exchanging them is the identity operation. There is nothing to permute.
That is why no check has caught this: the game is perfectly symmetric, and the index is harmless precisely
because nothing yet distinguishes the members. The first asymmetric per-team value breaks it. So the test
has to be run with a distinguishing value forced in, or it proves nothing.


WHERE THE SAME PROBLEM IS HIDDEN — blast radius, not repaired

Three of these are defects in the checks themselves, which I think is the more important half.

**The invention check is wrong in both directions for two members of one claim.** It finds the member a
recorded choice belongs to by searching for the first instantiation with a matching class id — and two
members of one claim share that id, so a choice about either always resolves to the first. It then stores
only the leaf *name*, not the value. So the first member's leaf is marked licensed and any value there
passes unchallenged, while the second member's correct value is reported as an invention. **The map ten
lines above it was explicitly hardened against exactly this** — its comment records the name-only hole —
and this one never got the same treatment. Latent, since no choice in A04 points into a member. This is
the finding I am least comfortable with, because it is a hole in the check everything else is trusted
against.

**The one joint-distinctness condition in the corpus never executes at all** — and this is independent of
identity. Its path is taken from the row it sits on, which is a field path (`objects[].position.along`)
rather than the collection, and the filter that uses it strips only a trailing `[]`. So nothing matches,
no tuples are collected, and the check passes having compared nothing. The identity degradation I went
looking for sits behind a defect that stops it running for anyone.

**GA-ROSTER-SUM cannot see asymmetry.** I forced 0 v 12 into A04 and it passes — correctly in its own
terms, since it checks the sum and 0 + 12 = 12. It cannot see that the equality which licensed the
division has been violated. **The game is still refused**, and it is worth saying what saved it: the check
that an entailed value survived into the game reports "entailed as 6 but the concrete game holds 0", and
render-eligibility comes back NO. So nothing wrong reaches a coach — but the invariant is weaker than it
looks, and the check that caught it is the value-based one, which is the shape the broken one is missing.

**A01, A02 and A05 carry two separate claims on the teams collection** and nothing sees they co-refer. Two
teams are refused whichever claim they are attributed to; **three teams draw no team or cardinality
objection at all.** The per-claim maximum counts only its own instantiations, and the collection-level
check runs before instantiation. Same error class as the three wide channels. **How latent: all three
goals cannot be realized at all today, and with that gate lifted the realization is still refused for five
unrelated reasons.** So it cannot reach a coach — but the guard that should stop it is silent, and the only
things in the way are unrelated to it.

**Per-team values are addressed by index**, and that is safe today only because the roster refuses unless
equality is authored — so a per-index number is written only where the index cannot matter. An equality
guard, not an identity guard: the safety is incidental.


7 · THE SECOND CORRECTION — member order is not deterministic

I told you member order is stable across runs. **I measured the wrong thing** — I checked the order of the
existential claims, which is derived and sorted. For instantiated members there is no sort anywhere in the
source, the index is assigned from the array length in the caller's order, and for A04 the only thing
fixing that order is the array order in the choices file, whose two entries are byte-identical. The
realization record's digest is the resolved game's input digest, so it does not cover realization's own
order-dependent decisions either.

So two members of one claim have **no content-derived ordering key at all.** That strengthens the finding
rather than weakening it: the index is not merely meaningless, it is not even anchored.

And one smaller correction on my own last note: the possession row carries a `fillableFrom` pointer to the
teams collection, and **no engine code reads `fillableFrom` anywhere.** The prose choice space is read, as
I said; the structural pointer is inert.


THE ONE RULING THIS NEEDS

The handle mechanism exists, the comparison kind that would consume it exists and is unused, and what is
missing is a mint for an element that no item individuates — derived from the claim that authorized the
member, plus a discriminator.

**The discriminator is yours, because two of your rulings meet there.** Position is an implementation
artifact and not authored football meaning; and identity may be required without carrying domain meaning.
Those are compatible only if **minting** is separated from **interpreting** — an ordinal may be an input
to minting an opaque handle, provided nothing afterwards can read it, order by it or recover it, which is
what the opacity requirement and the permutation test exist to enforce. The determinism finding sharpens
this: there is no stable ordinal to mint from today, so that reading also requires giving the
instantiation order a content-derived anchor.

So: is an ordinal permissible as an input to minting an opaque handle, with those guards? Or must the
derivation contain no ordinal at all — in which case identity cannot be established from the corpus, the
remaining sources being an authored distinguishing property or an opaque counter, which is an ordinal
wearing a hat. On that second reading **the two teams are genuinely indiscernible and the representation is
right to refuse to tell them apart**, and asymmetry and possession assignment are blocked on an authoring
decision.

One thing to weigh before choosing the second, because I only found it late: **that authoring route is not
currently open either.** As above, the `team` selector attribute is backed by no field row, so a
distinguishing team property would need a register row before any item could target it. So the conservative
reading is an authoring decision **plus** a register addition, not an authoring decision alone.

Both are coherent, and I have not chosen. Either way, given your C31b caution that asymmetry must remain
establishable, the ruling also decides whether a displacing game form can express what it displaces.

Nothing implemented. The touch trigger and the magnitude are untouched, and neither reading affects
either.
