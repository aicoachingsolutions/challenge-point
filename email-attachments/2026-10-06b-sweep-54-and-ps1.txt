0 · SD-44 NAMES THE RELATIONSHIP, NOT THE BALL — and I mis-stated it in writing

When I rewrote RC-19 on 5 October I put the prerequisite for a possession change as *"opposing teams and a ball
object"*. SD-44's own row says something different:

    "turnover when opposing teams and the relevant possession relationship exist"        (SD-44, evidence key C22)

**The relationship is the prerequisite. The ball is a prerequisite of the relationship.** Your ruling of 5 October
is consistent with that — *"possession is a relationship involving the ball ... cannot be considered structurally
reachable when the resolved game does not establish the prerequisites for that relationship"* — the prerequisites
*for the relationship*. So the chain is trigger → relationship → ball, and I collapsed it to trigger → ball when
I wrote the table.

The consequence is item 4 below: **the engine checks the deepest term and never checks the named one.**

**Measured rather than read.** With the ball's position withdrawn and with the withdrawal stripped in memory,
GA-ENVELOPE-FIT, GA-LAYOUT-FEASIBLE and GA-ONE-PRIMARY-EVENT return **identical verdicts** — on the corpus and on

So I adopted your wording, with the third conjunct about scoring references preserved and **no exception for any

**But the dependency is masked rather than absent, and you should have it on record.** Two gates genuinely require
an object position rather than merely checking one when present:

- **GA-ONE-PRIMARY-EVENT's position clause FAILS on an unpositioned referent** — `unpositioned.length ?
  fail(POSITION, …)`. If a ball were ever a referent of the primary scoring event, withdrawing its position would
  fail that clause outright.
- **GA-DIRECTION needs a derived interval on an object's along-row** to decide which end it is at. I know this
  first-hand rather than by reading: my first version of the applicability entry used a positive list, which
  withdrew the position of an object kind nobody had enumerated, and GA-DIRECTION went NOT_EVALUABLE. That is how
  the polarity bug was caught.

Neither bites today, because no ball is a primary-event referent or an objective's target. Both would bite the
moment one were. And GA-ENVELOPE-FIT does treat an unreadable placement as a reason to defer, so it will respond
to a withdrawn position once this corpus stops authoring placements as prose. All of that is now written into the
amended cell rather than left in correspondence.

**One defect found in passing, reported not repaired.** GA-ONE-PRIMARY-EVENT's position clause tests a referent's
identity one way and then resolves it another: the identity test accepts a typed structural reference, and the
resolution step then looks the referent up by class id, which a typed reference is not. **A typed reference passes
the identity test and then fails to resolve.** Same shape as the filter that could never match. Not touched.


4 · PS1 — THE TRACE, AND WHY A BALL IS NOT SUFFICIENT

**What was it intended to represent?** The *holder* of a relation the corpus was already naming. The register's own
note is unambiguous: *"the corpus named the states of this relation before anything could hold one. PS1 is what
holds it."* And the proposal that preceded it put the gap the same way: *"There is no field, on any row, for which
team has the ball."* Under your ruling of today that is squarely game structure — the rules key on it.

**What was supposed to establish it?** Nothing was in a position to. PS1 was added to the register on 3 October,
*after* all eight contracts had been restated against a register that had no such row. Measured: 860 declaration
reaches across 81 distinct rows, **zero of them on PS1**, and zero items anywhere carry that row — which is
exactly why its verdict reason is "no coverage", the engine's term for *nobody looked*.

**And this is not special to PS1, which I think matters more than the PS1 case itself.** All eight contracts
declare one identical frozen roster of 81 rows. The register now carries 90. **So there are rows the corpus
structurally cannot cover, because it was frozen against an older register** — PS1 is one of them, not a one-off.
The commit that added PS1 recorded the consequence in its own message: *"NOT_AUTHORED 24 → 25, both PS1."* It was
a known cost, not an oversight.

**Does POSSESSION_CHANGE require it?** Yes, and by your own ruling rather than by inference — SD-44 names *"the
relevant possession relationship"*, as in item 0.

**Why does reachability require a ball but not the relationship?** Because the engine checks a presupposition of
the prerequisite instead of the prerequisite. The trigger construction keys on two things, opposing teams and a
ball-kinded object, and **never reads PS1 at all.** A ball is what the relationship presupposes; the relationship
is what SD-44 names. So today a game can have the carrier, reach the trigger, realize cleanly — and still not hold
the relation the trigger exists to change. That is precisely what A01 with From Goal Kicks does.

**Why PS1 is not even offered as a choice, measured.** I traced the gate. PS1 fails the game-level existence gate:
a game-level row is only openable if the session sources it or something has *addressed* it, and PS1 is neither.
**So the obstacle is that its existence is unaddressed, not that its choice space is missing** — the choice space
is registered and fine. The threshold is low and worth knowing: *a single bounds-only contribution* would make the
line open, at which point SD-R2's "which team starts can remain a permitted free choice" becomes expressible; an
authored required contribution would resolve it outright.

**One provenance defect.** PS1's note cites **SD-R2, which is a retired non-authority** — explicitly listed as not
citable. The structurally identical row T2 was corrected away from exactly that citation and carries a
`fillableAuthority` naming SD-39 instead; PS1 carries no authority field at all. No code reads it, so nothing
behaves differently — but it is the same stale citation, left standing on the newer row.

**Recommendation, not implemented.** The smallest correction is a second named clause on GA-TRIGGER-REACHABLE:
where a transition is keyed on a possession change, the possession relation must be resolved or open. Not in the
trigger construction, because the trigger set is built before anything is resolved. I am not making it, for a
reason you should weigh: **it would fail a check that is green today, and it would mean a ball alone no longer
makes A04 realizable.** That is the right outcome if SD-44 means what it says, and it is a status change to a
frozen artifact, so it is yours.

**Which gives you a choice worth seeing plainly.** A ball alone restores reachability under the current
implementation and leaves the relation unestablished. A ball plus a bounds-only contribution on the possession
relation would satisfy SD-44 as written and let realization choose the starting team. The second is one item
larger and is the one I would take, but it is a knowledge decision and not mine.
