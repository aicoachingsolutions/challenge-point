A few notes on how I approached it, and three questions at the end that I think are genuinely yours.


WHAT IT IS

Five questions an authoring decision must answer before a new incentive meaning goes into canonical
knowledge: objective, legible, opposition-robust, affordance-preserving, proportionate. Each one gives the
question, what a pass and a failure look like, what evidence answers it, and — the part I found most useful
to write down — WHO can answer it. Some are machine-checkable, some are coaching judgement, and some are
yours alone. Recording which is which turned out to be about half the value.

I deliberately kept it to a checklist for a conversation. No tooling, no gate, nothing in the engine reads
it. You said you did not mean the broader Change Assurance System yet and I have taken that literally.


IT FORMALISES WHAT WE ALREADY HOLD

I did not import anything. The five are grounded in the incentive invariant from the framework doc
("incentives raise the value, attention and visibility of an opportunity — they must not script the
behavior"), in "invite, not force", in GF11's compactness-emergent finding, and in your scoring-ownership
rule. What is new is only that they are asked as questions, at authoring time, of a specific decision.

Worth naming the gap that makes this necessary: the guardrail currently lives as prose on library rows and
as wording checks in the communication layer. Those stop us PHRASING an incentive as an instruction. They
say nothing about whether its meaning is ecologically sound. That is the hole the criteria fill.


THE WORKED EXAMPLE, AND WHAT IT PRODUCED

I ran ball / player / touch through all five. I have not made the decision.

The criteria separate them clearly, and the separation is not what machine convenience would have chosen:

- PLAYER fails three criteria, and two of those failures are against AUTHORED knowledge rather than taste.
  A team can station a player in each channel and the condition is permanently satisfied — which
  contradicts the library's own audit for this row, "Zones optional", and the contract's own "Entering must
  not be compulsory". So it looks unavailable rather than merely unattractive.
- BALL passes the floor and is weak on proportionality for a reason that is quantitative: on A04's realized
  geometry the two channels are 50% of the pitch width, so mere ball presence is satisfied incidentally.
  ×2 on that makes the bonus the default and the base value close to decorative.
- TOUCH is strongest on the three criteria carrying the ecological intent, and its weakness is precisely
  the one the engine cares about most — a touch is a finer observation than a position.

Which is the tension you named, confirmed: the easiest reading to evaluate deterministically is not the one
the criteria favour. Had we chosen for convenience we would have chosen ball.

Four things the criteria do NOT settle and I have left to you: whose touch; "starting in" versus "moving
through" (the source authors two modes, and ruling on a subject does not resolve the mode); the episode
scope already returned separately; and whether ×2 is right once the condition is fixed.


ONE THING IT WOULD ALREADY HAVE CAUGHT

The framework carries an open question from Round 7.4 — whether an explicit attacker-side Transition Bonus
improves representativeness or over-scripts the race — with a note that it "should be resolved as part of
the incentive framework, not as a one-off". That is criterion 4 asked of a different decision. If the
criteria are adopted it has a procedure rather than a note.

2. Is a failure a veto or a finding? I wrote them as findings, because the player reading's failures look

3. Where do the answers live? I have suggested the restatement's own fitNote/basisEvidence, so the
