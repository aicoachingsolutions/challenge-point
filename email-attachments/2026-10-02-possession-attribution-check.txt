THE DECISIVE CASE — the Neutral Player Condition

A contracted object in the canonical corpus, nothing to do with Wide Zone. It authors on P6b, the row the
register defines as "team designation the neutral joins":

    P6b = ATTACKING_TEAM

and the restatement's own fitNote says what that means:

    "ATTACKING_TEAM ('in possession for the episode') is re-evaluated each episode; START and
     POSSESSION_CHANGE begin one (SD-14), so it holds 'currently in possession'."

A second P6b item puts it in plainer words still:

    "All neutrals become teammates of the team now in possession."

And P6a, the trigger on which affiliation is decided, is authored STANDING with:

    "a standing affiliation to ATTACKING_TEAM changes at every POSSESSION_CHANGE, which begins an episode."

So three things are settled by authored knowledge rather than by my reading of it:

1. **ATTACKING_TEAM is dynamic.** The restatement defines the token as "currently in possession". That
   settles the static-versus-dynamic question for the designation itself, not merely for neutrals.
2. **It is re-evaluated continuously**, at every possession change — not decided once at setup.
3. **A static assignment is not merely insufficient, it is forbidden.** The same object authors P6b
   excluding DEFENDING_TEAM ("forbids the team out of possession") and excluding TEAM_<id> with the reason
   "No neutral is fixed to one team."

That third point is what makes this decisive rather than arguable. We are not inferring that a fixed
designation would be inadequate; the knowledge says a fixed team is prohibited.


THE CONCEPT IS GENERAL, NOT LOCAL TO NEUTRALS

Seven register rows carry "team designation" as their value type: P6b (neutral affiliation), P9 (start
placement group), J3 (objective team), T1a and T1b (transition qualifiers), T2 (awardedTo) and V14a (value
consequence referents).

T2 is the sharpest of those after the neutrals, because the register states its evaluation time outright:

    T2 — "team designation evaluated at the trigger. N/A when playState is CONTINUE."

"Evaluated at the trigger" is dynamic by construction. A restart awarded at a POSSESSION_CHANGE cannot be
worked out in advance.

And POSSESSION_CHANGE is in the trigger vocabulary, used on the T1 row. Detecting that trigger at all
requires possession attribution, before anything is awarded on it.

So this is one general relationship that seven rows lean on, and it is currently defined in exactly one
place — a fitNote on a neutral-affiliation restatement.


SOMETHING I FOUND IN A04'S OWN ARTIFACT WHILE CHECKING

This bears on the question and I think you should see it.

A04's concrete game carries, on each instantiated team:

    designation: ATTACKING_TEAM        designation: DEFENDING_TEAM

Three problems with it:

- **There is no register row for `designation`.** The only rows the register owns under performers.teams[]
  are P2 (outfieldCount) and P3 (goalkeeper). So it is an unregistered property.
- **It has no authority.** It came from the realization choices file, and the stated reasons are purely
  positional: "First of the two" and "second of the two the claim asserts."
- **It sits against GF2's own authored objective.** GF2 authors J3 as "EACH_TEAM: one shared target attacked
  by both". If both teams attack the same target, statically labelling one of them the defending team is not
  a description of this game.

It passed the invention check because an instantiated member's own fields are authorised by the existential
claim that created it — the one blanket permission I left in place when I tightened that check, and it is
exactly where this slipped through.

So the only thing in A04 that resembles possession attribution today is an unregistered, authority-free,
static label. The requirement is not new; it is currently being met by an invention.


WHAT THIS MEANS FOR YOUR TWO OPTIONS

You wanted to avoid choosing between live possession attribution and a scorer-linked episode scope merely
because Wide Zone needs one. On the evidence, the choice is not Wide Zone's to force — and the two options
may not be as separate as they looked:

**SD-14 already ties episodes to possession.** The neutral restatement cites it for the proposition that
"START and POSSESSION_CHANGE begin an episode". So an episode boundary is already defined partly by a
possession change, and "the attacking team" is already defined as "in possession for the episode". The
episode concept and the possession concept are not two independent gaps. They are one relationship seen
twice, and the knowledge already states the link.

That suggests a single general relationship — the team in possession, per episode — would serve the neutral
affiliation, T2's awardedTo, the POSSESSION_CHANGE trigger, your Wide Zone condition and the episode scope
together. **I have not designed it and I have not implemented either mechanism**, as you instructed. I am
reporting that the narrower episode-scoped alternative and the possession-attribution option appear to be
two views of the same missing thing rather than genuine alternatives.
