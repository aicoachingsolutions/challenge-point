1. PATTERN 1 — DONE

Adopted as a general applicability rule, not a special case. POSSESSION_CHANGE
is named nowhere in it; it simply fails every out-of-play condition.

The existing applicability mechanism could only condition on a governing LINE,
which is resolved at stage 6. A trigger is fixed by the selector when stage 2
individuates the class, so the grammar now also accepts a selector attribute,
and such a line is withdrawn at enumeration rather than left conditional on
something that can never move.

One design point I want to flag because it is the safeguard. Where the selector
does not fix the attribute, the condition is UNDECIDABLE and the line is KEPT.
So this rule can only ever remove a line it can positively show does not belong.
It cannot hide a real gap behind an applicability rule.

Implementing the invariant took a second correction. Withdrawing the lines was
not enough — they were still carrying a NOT_AUTHORED verdict and still emitting
a GAP record, so they were reported as inapplicable and counted as missing
knowledge at the same time. A withdrawn line now carries no verdict and emits no
GAP.

  unestablished lines      57 -> 45
  NOT_AUTHORED             54 -> 45
  GAP records              54 -> 45
  withdrawn                12 -> 21

You asked whether any other transition/property combination exposes the same
issue. The sweep found one, and I have left it failing rather than deciding it:

T1c (qualifiers.region) on the goal kick, which is OUT_END_LINE. I deliberately
wrote T1c's rule wider than strictly necessary — it applies to OUT_END_LINE,
OUT_TOUCHLINE and REGION_ENTRY — because the register's own triggerQualifiers
block says REGION_ENTRY takes a region reference, and a ball that leaves the
field leaves it somewhere. So the goal kick keeps the line and it still fails.
Whether an OUT_END_LINE transition needs a region qualifier when T1b already
says whose end line, and T4 already holds "where the ball went out", is a
question I did not want to answer by narrowing my own rule to make a line
disappear.

No other combination on the corpus shows the pattern.
