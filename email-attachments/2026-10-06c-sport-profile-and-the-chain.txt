1. THE MINIMAL SOCCER SPORT PROFILE

Two items, nothing else:

  - the game's functional object is a ball (row O1, selector kind=ball, EXISTS, REQUIRED)
  - the possession relationship is a valid team-valued relationship over the participating opposing
    teams and that ball (row PS1, RANGE, REQUIRED_RANGE)

Both carry your owner ruling as their basis. No Laws of the Game, no field dimensions, no player
counts, no starting holder, no layout position for the ball. Each omission is written into the profile
as a deliberate non-contribution with its reason, so a later reader can tell silence from oversight.

It enters through the existing contribution-contract pathway and uses no new mechanism. Its items are
graded by exactly the same rules as any other item: the ball establishes an element because the
grading admits an owner ruling, not because the file is special.

It is loaded separately from the eight restated objects, and that is structural rather than tidiness.
Those eight are what a goal SELECTS from, and each is frozen against the 81-row roster of its own
restatement. A Sport Profile is selected by nobody — it is the environment every activity of this
sport runs in — so it reaches every derivation unconditionally. Putting it in the same file would have
made it look selectable and would have put a new contribution inside a frozen set.

One thing I want to flag because it caught me: the first version of the loader had the word "soccer"
written into the engine, and the project's sport-coupling guard failed the build on the first run.
Neither of the two remedies that guard offers was right — declaring the engine a sport layer, or moving
a knowledge-directory read into the sport module. The right answer was a third one: the engine does not
name the sport at all. It finds the profile by pattern in the knowledge directory, so the sport's
identity lives in the artefact. Absence and plurality both stop the run rather than resolve quietly —
an empty directory must not hand back nothing and let the ball disappear, and two profiles is a choice
of sport the engine is not entitled to make by sort order.


2. THE POSSESSION CONTRIBUTION IS BOUNDS-ONLY BY CONSTRUCTION, NOT BY INTENTION

This is the part I checked hardest, because "bounds-only" is the sort of claim that is true on the day
it is written and quietly stops being true later.

It cannot entail. The requirement form it uses is outside the set the engine treats as entailing, and
its value is a qualitative term rather than an enumerated set, so the narrowing path declines it too.
There is exactly one place left for it to land, and that is the bounding channel. I also checked the
obvious alternative: a bare existence assertion is inert on a field row, so it was never available.

The consequence is the one you asked for. The relationship is established; who holds it is not. PS1
comes out as an authorized free choice, and realization supplies an initial holder as a recorded
decision rather than as knowledge. No team is named anywhere in the profile — I checked for that
explicitly, because it is the one line in your authorization I was most likely to cross by accident.

The chosen holder is reported as UNVERIFIED rather than as checked, because a qualitative bound cannot
be compared to a value. That grading is now asserted by name, so a future unverifiable choice cannot
appear quietly alongside the ones that were genuinely checked.


3. THE CHAIN, ENFORCED AND PROVED FROM BOTH ENDS

GA-TRIGGER-REACHABLE now carries your second clause: where a transition is keyed on a possession
change, the relevant possession relationship must be established or legitimately open.

I did not want to report that as working on the strength of a run where everything is present, because
a chain that only ever runs intact is indistinguishable from the collapsed version it replaced. So both
halves are tested by removal:

  - ball kept, relationship removed  ->  first clause PASSES, second clause FAILS, the gate fails.
    Your sentence holds: the ball alone no longer makes a possession change reachable.

  - relationship kept, ball removed  ->  first clause FAILS, because the trigger is never constructed
    at all. Second clause passes.

Neither test alone is your ruling. Together they are the chain, and the comment in the code says so, so
the next person to simplify it has to argue with it first.

One consequence I did not anticipate and think is worth having. Until today the only thing supplying a
ball to any real game was a line authored inside ONE practice situation — A01's goal kicks. So A01 with
that situation could reach a turnover and A01 without it could not: whether the game's turnover was
possible depended on which situation a coach had picked. That is now gone. Both forms of A01 reach the
trigger, because the carrier is a fact about the sport rather than a fact about a planning choice. The
situation's own authored ball is still there and still admitted; it is simply no longer load-bearing,
which is the right relationship between a sport fact and a practice situation.


4. THE CLAUSE'S FIRST REAL SUBJECT IS YOUR OWN CORPUS, AND IT FAILS

I ran it against the full stage-B corpus — all eight restated objects together, not a bounded

So the corpus has been declaring turnovers with nothing holding the relation a turnover changes. Not

recorded that as the expected state rather than repairing anything, because what to do about it is a

And one finding that answers a question you did not ask but would have:

GA-NO-FAILED-LINE would never have caught this, and could not. Its blocking filter admits an
unestablished line only where the reason is a declared gap or a claim left unresolved. "No coverage"
is counted among the established absences and passes. I measured it four ways to be sure: with the
relationship unestablished AND unclaimed it passes and counts the row as unspoken; with the row
claimed but unestablished it fails. A row that no object claims is invisible to that check. That is
why this obligation had to be a reachability clause rather than a tightening of the failed-line check,
and it is worth knowing in general: our strongest check against missing values says nothing about a
row nobody has looked at.

8. TWO DEFECTS OF MY OWN, FOUND AND FIXED

Both are the project's recurring failure in miniature, so I would rather you had them than not.

The first: my new clause read the possession line directly and only recorded it when the clause FAILED.
So a passing report named nothing, and there was no way to tell from it whether the clause had run at
all. A check that leaves no trace when it passes is a check nobody can confirm is still working. It now
reads the line through the same mechanism every other check uses, and a test asserts the line is named
on a PASSING run.

The second is worse and I want to be plain about it. The initial possession holder was recorded against
an ordinal that does not exist — the realizer numbers members from one, and the choice named zero. So
the one realized game on the pilot path carried a reference to a team that is not in it. Three
independent things had to line up for that to pass, and all three did: the bound is qualitative, so
nothing compared the value to anything; nothing yet reads the possession value, so nothing downstream
tripped over it; and the acceptance account correctly reported nothing lost and nothing invented,
because a dangling reference is neither. A test for each of those three would still have passed.

Fixed, and more usefully: realization now refuses a chosen value that has the shape of a member
reference and names no member of the game. An unverifiable bound means the TERM cannot be checked. It
does not mean any string will do.

A third one, which I mention because the near-miss is instructive rather than because anything broke.
One of our baselines records, for each of the thirteen goals, how many required properties are left
unestablished. After today's change every one of those thirteen numbers is unchanged — which reads as
perfect containment, and I was about to report it that way. The membership had swapped underneath:
the possession relationship left the unestablished set, and the ball's COUNT arrived in it, because
the profile deliberately does not say how many balls there are. One out, one in, in every goal, with
the total steady. The exchange is legitimate. The baseline that could not see it was not, and it now
names both lines instead of trusting the total.
