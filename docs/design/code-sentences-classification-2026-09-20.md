These are only the sentences the conformance check actually depended on. Each row gives what the

## 1–4. The four "beyond the first defenders" setups

| # | Sentence | Where |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Mark a line across the pitch beyond the first defenders." | coach rule, RPC-001 with line crossed |
| 2 | "Mark a target zone beyond the first defenders." | coach rule, RPC-001 with target zone entered |
| 3 | "Set out gates of two cones beyond the first defenders." | coach rule, RPC-001 with gates |
| 4 | "Place a target player beyond the first defenders." | coach rule, RPC-001 with target player |

**What rested on them.** RPC-001-04 (where the defending team starts), RPC-001-05 (the target player
role and its placement) and RPC-001-09 (the scoring zone and gates). In the check these were the items
that placed RPC-001's scoring carrier.

**What the knowledge already authors.** RPC-PROP-132: "Initiated from the goalkeeper or a controlled
defensive restart, progressing beyond the initial pressure or progression line, with possession
controlled on arrival." That is a **condition on the scoring event**, in terms of a progression line —
not an instruction to lay out a carrier, and not in terms of defenders.

**Recommendation: retire all four as legacy realization wording.**
- His ruling KR-04 already says RPC-001 does not own or instantiate the carrier. These sentences do
  precisely that on its behalf.
- "The first defenders" is a formation of players during play, outside the representation's boundary.
  The authored term, the initial pressure or progression line, is a region and is representable.
- The scoring condition survives without them, because RPC-PROP-132 authors it.

**The gap this leaves, honestly.** Nothing then authors **where a carrier sits relative to the
progression line**. That belongs to whoever owns carriers — a Game Form or a realization — and it is a
knowledge-authoring question, not a grammar one. Today no Game Form authors it.

## 5. The scoring rule, which carries two distinct claims

> "Earn a point when a player dribbles over the line, or receives the ball past it and controls it."
> "A long kick that lands past the line does not count."

**5a — the event definition.** Authored already: the scoring-event vocabulary defines the event, and
RPC-PROP-132 gives its conditions. **Retire the sentence as wording.**

**5b — one point.** No workbook authors any base value for a scoring event; the only point-value text in
the knowledge is a realization example about targets carrying different values. RPC-001-20 rested
entirely on this sentence. **Recommendation: a standing decision** — one point per qualifying event,
unless a selected object authors otherwise. It is a scoring convention rather than football knowledge,
which is why I do not propose authoring it as domain knowledge. If he would rather author it, that works
equally well; what it cannot be is left in code.

**5c — the long-kick exclusion.** Authored already, as the final clause of RPC-PROP-132: "A long
clearance that happens to reach the target does not satisfy the event." **Retire the sentence.**

**Flag on SD-15.** His ruling named "long kick". The authored term is **"long clearance"**. In the check
this exact mismatch made RPC-001's exclusion item report as violated, because matching is verbatim. I
recommend SD-15's free qualitative judgement attach to the authored term, with "long kick" treated as
coach-facing rendering of it.

## 6. The build-out start

> "Start each attack from your goalkeeper or a restart in your own half."

**What rested on it.** RPC-001-06, and RPC-001-16 — the rule that turnovers stop play, which his SD-20
and KR-03 have already unsupported.

**What the knowledge authors.** RPC-PROP-001: "Goalkeeper controls possession or play restarts from a
controlled defensive restart." That is the begin condition, and it stands on its own.

**Recommendation: retire as legacy realization wording.** Two specifics make this more than tidying:
- **"each attack" contradicts KR-03.** He ruled that RPC-001's build-out requirement "applies to the
  build-out episode, not automatically to every attacking episode in the activity". The sentence says
  every attack. It is appended to every RPC-001 rule and setup the runtime produces.
- **"in your own half" is an additional placement claim** that no workbook authors. RPC-PROP-001 says
  nothing about halves.

**The gap this leaves.** Where the restart is taken from, when a build-out restart is used. That is
authoring, and it is his.
