Challenge Point™ Coach Communication Standard
RC2
Status: Release Candidate 2 Owner: Experience Architecture Purpose: Coach-Facing Activity Communication
Purpose
The Coach Communication Standard defines how Challenge Point communicates generated activities to coaches.
Its purpose is not to explain the educational theory behind representative learning.
Its purpose is to enable coaches to confidently organize, understand, and begin an activity with minimal cognitive effort.
Challenge Point should communicate activities in the same way experienced coaches naturally exchange practice ideas—with clarity, brevity, and immediate usability.
Design Philosophy
The representative learning architecture performs the educational reasoning.
The coach-facing activity should communicate only the information required to successfully organize and facilitate the activity.
The activity should answer the coach's practical questions—not reveal the engine's reasoning.
Communication Principles
Principle 1 — Coach Workflow Over Engine Workflow
Activity descriptions should follow the order in which a coach naturally prepares practice.
What are we working on?
How do I set it up?
How do we play?
How do teams score?
When does it end?
The communication structure should mirror the coach's workflow rather than the generation process.
Principle 2 — Communicate How to Play
Describe the game.
Do not describe the learning process.
Avoid explaining:
decisions
perceptions
representative intentions
tactical reasoning
These should emerge naturally from the activity itself.
Principle 3 — Minimize Coach Translation
Every additional sentence increases cognitive effort.
The objective is to minimize translation between reading and coaching.
A coach should spend their attention organizing players rather than interpreting text.
Principle 4 — Show the Environment
Describe the environment.
Avoid describing player cognition.
Prefer:
Play begins with the goalkeeper.
Instead of:
Players decide when to secure possession.
Principle 5 — Simplicity Supports Confidence
Activities should feel immediately runnable.
If coaches hesitate because they are still interpreting the activity, communication should be simplified.
The One Question Per Section Standard
Each section owns one coaching question.
Section
Coach Question
Objective
What are we working on?
Setup
How do I organize it?
Rules
How does play work?
Scoring
How do teams score?
Win Condition
When does the activity end?
No section should answer another section's question.
The One Read Rule
A coach unfamiliar with the activity should understand the activity after one reading.
They should immediately understand:
objective
setup
rules
scoring
without rereading.
Target reading time:
30 seconds or less
The Sentence Test
Every sentence should answer one question:
Does this sentence help the coach run the activity?
If the answer is No,
remove it.
The Coach Translation Test
For every sentence ask:
Does the coach have to mentally translate this into an action?
If Yes,
rewrite it.
Examples:
Instead of:
Players decide when to switch play.
Write:
Play continues until possession changes.
Language Standards
Prefer
Observable actions.
Examples:
Play starts with the goalkeeper.
Restart with a goal kick.
Attack the end zone.
Play continues after every turnover.
Avoid
Interpretive language.
Examples:
Players decide...
Teams aim to...
Session focus...
Immediate attacking advantage...
Players recognize...
Players choose...
The environment should create these behaviors rather than the activity describing them.
Scoring Standard
Scoring should answer only one question:
How are points earned?
Scoring should not explain:
tactical reasoning
representative intention
environmental consequences
If those are important,
they belong elsewhere.
Communication Hierarchy
When simplifying communication, preserve information in the following order.
Safety
Setup
Rules
Scoring
Educational explanation
Educational explanation should always be sacrificed first.
Appendix A
Coach Communication Transformation Framework
Every activity should pass through the following editing process before presentation.
Step 1
Identify the section.
Objective
Setup
Rules
Scoring
Win Condition
Step 2
Determine the coaching question.
Example:
Objective
"What are we working on?"
Step 3
Remove everything that does not answer that question.
Step 4
Rewrite using observable coaching language.
Step 5
Read the section aloud.
If it sounds like something an experienced coach would naturally say,
it is probably appropriate.
If it sounds like documentation,
simplify it.
Appendix B
Before and After Examples
Example 1 — Objective
Current
Secure possession and progress within the time constraint. Decide when to switch play or maintain possession based on time and space. Session focus: Maintain possession with forward intent.
Improved
Build out from the back against high pressure by maintaining possession and progressing into the opponent's end zone.
Why
Removes:
duplicated concepts
player cognition
session explanation
States only the coaching intention.
Example 2 — Setup
Current
Play 9v9 in a 40x30 yard area with two 20-yard end zones. Teams aim to maintain possession and progress towards the opposing end zone within a set time limit. Start with a kickoff from the center. Play restarts from the center after a score or when the ball goes out.
Improved
9v9 + GKs. 40 × 30 m field with two end zones and a central channel. Play starts with the goalkeeper. Restart with a goal kick or from the end zone.
Why
Separates:
field organization
restart procedure
Removes learning explanation.
Example 3 — Rules
Current
Play stays live as possession is secured and progressed toward the target under pressure. A forced ball or turnover flips the immediate attacking advantage to the opponent with no reset.
Improved
Teams attack by progressing into the opponent's end zone. If possession changes, play continues immediately with no reset.
Why
Describes observable play.
Removes internal terminology.
Example 4 — Scoring
Current
Earn a point for passes or runs that break or bypass a defensive line; line-breaking attempts that are read and intercepted hand the advantage to the opponent on the regain. Earn an extra point for switching play across the field.
Improved
Attack: 1 point for reaching the opponent's end zone. Defend: 1 point for winning possession or forcing a turnover.
Why
Scoring should answer only one question:
How do we get points?
Everything else belongs in the activity design—not the coach-facing communication.
Success Criteria
Coach communication is successful when a coach can:
understand the activity after one read
organize the activity without interpretation
explain the activity to players without rereading
begin play within minutes
The quality of coach-facing communication should be measured by how little translation it requires between reading the activity and running the activity.
