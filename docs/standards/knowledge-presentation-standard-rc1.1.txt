Challenge Point™ Knowledge Presentation Standard RC1.1
Status: Constitutional Platform Standard
1. Purpose
The purpose of this standard is to govern how Challenge Point presents internally generated knowledge to coaches.
Challenge Point's reasoning architecture exists to produce high-quality decisions from multiple interacting knowledge systems. Coaches, however, should experience those decisions as clear, concise, and immediately actionable guidance.
This standard establishes the principles, ownership boundaries, and presentation contracts that govern every coach-facing output produced by the platform.
Its purpose is not to define reasoning.
Its purpose is not to define coaching knowledge.
Its purpose is to define how the results of reasoning become communication.
2. Scope
This standard applies to every coach-facing output generated by Challenge Point.
Including, but not limited to:
Generated Activities
Activity Adjustments
Live Coaching Guidance
Session Recommendations
Practice Planning
Reflection Summaries
Learning Progress Feedback
Future AI-assisted coaching interactions
This standard applies regardless of sport.

4. Communication Philosophy
Challenge Point should reveal its intelligence through clarity rather than completeness.
The platform may reason over hundreds of interacting knowledge objects, relationships, probabilities, and validations.
The coach should experience only the information necessary to confidently act.
The measure of intelligence is therefore not how much the platform communicates.
It is how little the coach must think about the communication before understanding what to do.
5. Ownership
Knowledge Presentation owns:
coach-facing structure
information hierarchy
section purpose
presentation consistency
communication contracts
coach vocabulary expectations
Knowledge Presentation does not own:
reasoning
validation
decision making
knowledge generation
activity selection
representative assessment
6. Presentation Pipeline
The platform communicates through successive transformations.
Knowledge
↓
Reasoning
↓
Communication Contributions
↓
Composition
↓
Translation
↓
Coach Presentation
Each stage has a distinct purpose.
Knowledge supplies facts.
Reasoning determines relevance.
Communication Contributions expose candidate information.
Composition determines what should ultimately be communicated.
Translation expresses that information in coach language.
Presentation delivers the final experience.
Each stage should reduce complexity.
No stage should increase it.
7. Communication Principles
Principle 1
Communicate Decisions, Not Reasoning
Coaches need conclusions.
They do not need the reasoning process that produced those conclusions.
Internal reasoning should remain internal unless explicitly requested.
Principle 2
Every Sentence Must Earn Its Place
Within each presentation level, every communicated sentence should provide new information.
Within each presentation level, repeated ideas reduce clarity without increasing understanding.
When two statements in the same presentation level communicate substantially the same idea, only the strongest expression should remain.
Principle 3
Communicate Only Actionable Information
Coach-facing communication should enable action.
Information that cannot influence coaching behaviour should not normally be presented.
Principle 4
Respect Coach Expertise
Challenge Point assumes coaches understand the game.
The platform should avoid explaining concepts that competent coaches already understand.
Only distinctions that influence the current activity should be communicated.
Principle 5
Concrete Before Abstract
Concrete coaching instructions take precedence over conceptual descriptions.
Examples:
Preferred
"Score by dribbling or passing into the end zone."
Less Preferred
"Target progression remains active."
Principle 6
One Purpose Per Section
Each section of an activity has one responsibility.
Information should appear where coaches naturally expect it.
Within each presentation level, sections should not compete for ownership of the same idea.
Principle 7
Internal Vocabulary Remains Internal
Architectural terminology should never appear in coach-facing communication.
Examples include:
Representative Validation
Environmental Manipulation
Interaction Regulation
Affordance
Game Problem
Runtime Assessment
Published Adjustment Option
These concepts guide reasoning rather than communication.
8. Presentation Contracts
Every coach-facing output should satisfy the following contracts.
Clarity
A coach should understand the activity after one reading.
Brevity
Communication should be no longer than necessary.
Consistency
Equivalent knowledge should be presented consistently across all generated activities.
Non-Redundancy
Within each presentation level, concepts should not be restated across multiple sections. A concise Coach Overview may appropriately summarize information also available in Coach Details.
Coach Language
Communication should read as though written by an experienced coach rather than assembled by software.
Immediate Usability
A coach should be able to run the activity without mentally translating system terminology.
9. Quality Standards
Before presentation, generated outputs should satisfy:
✓ Appropriate information ownership
✓ No duplicated concepts within each presentation level
✓ No architectural terminology
✓ No implementation language
✓ No metadata presented as coach instructions
✓ Section responsibilities preserved
✓ Language appropriate for the selected coaching level
✓ Information ordered naturally
10. Success Measures
A successful presentation enables a coach to:
understand the activity within one minute
understand the activity well enough to explain it to players concisely in their own language; Challenge Point should not provide or prescribe a player-facing script
identify the primary learning objective immediately
organize the activity without consulting implementation details
focus attention on coaching rather than interpretation
The communication should feel intentionally written rather than mechanically assembled.
11. Relationship to Coach Intelligence
Coach Intelligence determines:
what should be communicated
Knowledge Presentation determines:
how that communication is presented
The separation is intentional.
Coach Intelligence owns coaching decisions.
Knowledge Presentation owns coach experience.
12. Relationship to Future Development
This standard governs communication independently of sport-specific knowledge.
Soccer, basketball, rugby, volleyball, and future sports should all communicate according to the same principles despite differing knowledge bases.
As new reasoning systems are introduced, they should contribute information through the existing presentation contracts rather than bypassing them.
The evolution of Challenge Point should increase the quality of reasoning while decreasing the cognitive effort required to understand its outputs.
Closing Statement
Challenge Point exists to support better coaching, not to display sophisticated reasoning.
The platform's intelligence should be evident in the quality of its guidance rather than the visibility of its architecture.
As the platform's internal reasoning grows more capable, its coach-facing communication should become simpler, clearer, and more natural.
