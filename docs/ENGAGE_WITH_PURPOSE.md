## His principle (his second message filed 26 October 2026, 9:56 AM)

The message as he wrote it, without its greeting and sign-off:

> One additional thought following my previous email that I think is worth emphasizing.
>
> We've discussed how opposition incentives shape the affordances available to players, but they serve another equally important function: motivating players to engage with energy, intensity, and purpose.
>
> This is fundamental to Challenge Point's philosophy. An activity can be exceptionally well designed from a representative learning perspective, but if players aren't sufficiently engaged, much of its learning potential is lost.
>
> The physical-goal example illustrates both functions particularly well. A goalkeeper-protected goal creates more representative consequences for losing possession, but it also introduces something players naturally find exciting: the opportunity to score goals and the challenge of preventing them.
>
> These are meaningful, game-integrated incentives that can stimulate competitive intensity without relying on artificial rewards, punishments, or restrictive rules.
>
> I want Challenge Point to recognize both dimensions when selecting and evaluating opposition incentives:
>
> Learning value: How does the incentive shape the affordances, interactions, and consequences relevant to the Learning Goal?
>
> Engagement value: Does the incentive create a compelling competitive problem that motivates players to participate actively and purposefully?
>
> Both are important. Engagement isn't merely an added benefit of a well-designed learning environment; it's part of what makes that environment effective.
>
> This isn't a request for additional implementation work right now, but I do want the principle preserved as we continue developing the knowledge architecture.
>
> It connects directly to our broader philosophy: Engage with Purpose.

## The two values, as questions to ask of an opposition incentive

| Value | His question |
|---|---|
| Learning value | "How does the incentive shape the affordances, interactions, and consequences relevant to the Learning Goal?" |
| Engagement value | "Does the incentive create a compelling competitive problem that motivates players to participate actively and purposefully?" |

"Both are important. Engagement isn't merely an added benefit of a well-designed learning environment; it's part of what makes that environment effective."

## He reinforced it the next day (his reply filed 27 October 2026, item 8)

> I also want to reinforce the motivational consideration from my follow-up email.
>
> An opposition objective needs to do more than shape affordances. It should help create an energetic, competitive environment in which players genuinely want to succeed.
>
> A physical goal and goalkeeper can make regains more exciting and defending possession more consequential.
>
> But the activity still needs to provide enough attainable opportunities for both teams to remain engaged.
>
> As we evaluate the first realization, I want us to consider both its representative learning value and its ability to sustain purposeful competitive intensity.

## Where it sits in the knowledge architecture (ours)

- **Beside the Incentive Assurance Criteria** (`INCENTIVE_ASSURANCE_CRITERIA.md`, approved 2 October 2026), which govern
  every incentive authoring decision and are read before any incentive work. The seven approved criteria are unchanged by
  this document. They ask whether an incentive's meaning is ecologically sound; criterion 6, Learning-Relevant, is the
  nearest of them to his learning value. None of them asks his engagement question. Whether engagement value becomes a
  criterion, and how the two values are weighed against each other, is his to decide; nothing here decides it.
- **Beside the incentive framework** (`CONSTRAINT_INCENTIVE_FRAMEWORK.md`, Part 2), which maps how incentives influence
  exploration, exploitation, attention, risk and tactical priorities. Engagement value is not one of those five influence
  dimensions; it is the second function he names for an opposition incentive, beside shaping affordances.
- **Until he rules further** (OUR READING of his words "I want Challenge Point to recognize both dimensions when selecting and evaluating opposition incentives:"):
  when an opposition incentive is next selected or evaluated, both of his questions
  are asked and answered in prose in the decision's record, beside the seven criteria, as the criteria's own answers are
  recorded (the authored item's `fitNote` / `basisEvidence`). No mechanism enforces this, and none is to be built without
  his request.
- **First use**: the field-test observation sheet for the goal version of Play Out from the Back
  (`field-test/play-out-from-the-back-observation-sheet.txt`) asks his engagement questions on the field, questions 9 and
