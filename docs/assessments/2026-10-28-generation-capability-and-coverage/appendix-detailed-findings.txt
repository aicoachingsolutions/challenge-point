1. METHOD, TERMS AND CAVEATS

How this was measured
- The findings come from running the built application as it stood before your 28 October Part 1 refinements: its routes (the coach review page and the live generation route), and for the reuse tests the engine function those routes call. Reading the code supplements this in places. The database and the AI service were simulated, and nothing connected to the outside. No code or knowledge was changed; the reuse tests used temporary test-only copies of knowledge.
- Play Out from the Back: 34 sessions, each changing one input. All 13 Learning Goals: 304 sessions. These covered every Practice Situation, Learning Stage and Challenge Level, a note, three durations, and 10, 12 or 14 players with 0, 1 or 2 goalkeepers. Reuse: the two Play Out from the Back versions were copied onto four other goals, and games without roles were tested for a fifth goal (section 5).
- A second, independent run checked the coverage results and the reuse tests. Where it corrected the first run, this document gives the corrected finding. The Play Out from the Back facts were measured in two separate runs.
- No real AI call was made. What the live app does with an AI answer was tested with a simulated, well-formed answer.
- The Part 1 refinements change wording, how the goalkeeper may restart, the equipment list, the marked line's range and the zone version's goalkeeper handling area. None of this changes the number of activities. They also add a general check that keeps the marked line outside the penalty area; the reuse tests with goal versions were not re-run with it.

Terms
- Checked pathway: the knowledge-driven engine. It builds the game only from authored knowledge, runs every structural check, and tells the coach only what the game holds.
- AI-written pathway (the legacy pathway in your message): code picks a game form, two to four library constraints and a scoring sentence. An AI language model (GPT-4o) then writes the activity. Only its format and basic mechanics are checked.
- Engine-readable: a knowledge entry (a context, a game form or a constraint) restated in the engine's terms so the engine can build from it. We have called this a contribution contract.
- Relationship: a confirmed link in your context library that permits a departure from the game form's normal rules. For example, the pressing team may score in a goal.
- Authorization: your approval, for one goal, of the context, Game Problem, constraints and versions that apply. Only Play Out from the Back has one.
- Version: a fully specified form of an activity. Play Out from the Back has two: the goal version and the zone version.
- Coach wording: the sentences the engine uses to describe each part of a game to the coach, approved by you or drafted for your review. Anything without a sentence is refused.
- Two-to-four rule: an authorization that names any library constraint must name two to four, with a required mix of constraint roles (for example, foundation and consequence constraints).
- Geometry-check defect: an engine fault in the check that positions measured from an end line still hold once a penalty area is marked out (details in 5.4).

Labels: each gap is tagged by what would close it, the same way as in the main document.
- [knowledge]: soccer knowledge not yet engine-readable or not yet decided. A knowledge entry, or your ruling or approval, closes it, with no new code.
- [knowledge: our draft, needs your ruling]: an interpretation we recorded that the engine relies on. Your ruling plus a knowledge entry changes it, with no new code.
- [engine]: engine code that is missing or needs correcting.
- [both]: needs both.


2. PLAY OUT FROM THE BACK: VARIETY (your item 12)

In short: one activity structure, the build-out-versus-press game on Directional Possession Games, in two equipment versions. The same session gets the identical activity every time. This is your "only one activity with several configurations" case.

Resource alternatives: 2
- Goal version. Used when the coach lists an FA 5v5 goal (12 x 6 ft) and has a goalkeeper. The pressing team attacks the goal, the goal's penalty area is marked, and the teams change ends at each new block.
- Zone version. Used with no goal, a full-size goal, a mini-goal or a goal of another width. The pressing team attacks a 5 m zone and also scores by a controlled regain inside it. The teams keep their ends and exchange roles.
- The versions differ in the pressing team's scoring as well as in equipment. They come as a package: a coach cannot choose the incentive separately.

Parameter adjustments: very few are served
- Players: only 11 or 12. At a new block, the goalkeeper changes team at 11 players; at 12, the goalkeeper and one outfield player do. Sessions of 8, 9, 10, 13, 14, 15 and 16 players are refused: "The team set-up for this activity has not been decided for [N] players."
- Area: only 40 x 30 m. Areas of 30 x 20, 45 x 30, 50 x 35 and 60 x 40 m are refused: "The starting positions for this activity have not been decided for a [size] area."
- Goalkeepers: a session with none is refused. With two available there is still one active goalkeeper. Only the wording of the team make-up changes.
- Duration (15, 20 or 30 minutes) changes only the playing time.
- Challenge Level: low, medium and high give the identical activity.
- The text suggests four coach adjustments: add a pressing player, remove a build-out player, move the marked line, change starting positions. The engine generates none of them. Some, such as a different number of players, would take the session outside what is served.

Constraint and incentive variations: 0
- Your authorization for this goal names no library constraint, so none is selected. Your rulings exclude Wide Zone Advantage and the Pass Combination Gate, and the empty authorization sets aside every other candidate.
- Goalkeeper Build-Out also approves scoring by crossing a line, through a gate, or to a target player. No input selects any of them. The line option is withheld for two reasons: no approved wording describes it, and describing it would need facts the game does not hold.
- The equipment choice switches the pressing team's scoring between the goal and the zone with its regain score. Apart from that, no input changes the opposition's opportunities, the scoring consequences or the information available to players.

Distinct activity structures: 1
- Both versions are the same build-out-versus-press structure on Directional Possession Games.
- Positional Play Games and Channel Games are also candidates for this goal. The code that chooses the game form picks Directional Possession Games at every Challenge Level, and the coach cannot choose another. Neither of the other two is engine-readable, so the checked engine would refuse them anyway.

Inputs (details in section 4c): Against High Pressure, Through Central Areas and Through Wide Areas give the identical activity, so central and wide build-outs produce the same game. From Goal Kicks is refused. If the coach has not said what goals they have, the app asks.

Finding 2: one activity, two equipment versions
  What works: a complete, checked, authorized activity in two equipment versions. The version is chosen correctly from what the coach has, and it is identical on the review page and in the live app.
  What doesn't: there is one structure and no selectable constraint or incentive variation. Only 40 x 30 m and 11-12 players are served. Situation, stage, challenge and note do not change the activity.
  Why it matters: a coach who chooses this goal on two different days with the same session gets the same activity. The engine is serving one authored activity, not generating activities.
  Blocks a pilot? It does not block the field test of this activity. It does block a pilot of the application, which needs meaningful choice.
  Decision needed: what each Practice Situation should change (for example, what should Through Wide Areas do differently?), and whether to widen this goal now or unlock other goals first (section 7).

5. REUSE OF THE RECENT MECHANISMS (your item 14)

How the reuse was tested
- In test-only knowledge, the two Play Out from the Back versions were copied onto the game forms of four other goals: Stay Organized, Defend 1v1 and Progress the Attack on Directional Possession Games, and Win the Ball Back on Transition Games. Progress the Attack also stood in for Finishing and Create Scoring Chances, whose game forms are not engine-readable. For Secure Possession, games without roles were tested instead.
- Every copy was run through the engine function that the review page and the live route call.

Two caveats from the independent check apply to every result
1. To get past the first refusal, the test had to leave out the goal's context. A "served" result therefore shows that the mechanism works for another goal's shape, not that a valid activity for that goal exists. For example, the served Stay Organized and Defend 1v1 copies score by entering a zone, which their context (Attack Prevention) does not approve. With the context kept, the engine would refuse them.
2. Those copies are build-out games served under defending goals, and nothing in the engine objected (section 6, gap 11).

5.1 Each team scoring its own way (role-specific scoring)
Tested on: Stay Organized; Progress the Attack, also standing in for Finishing; Win the Ball Back; and Stay Organized with renamed roles.
Result: PARTIAL. The scoring check itself works for another goal.
- Pressing team scoring in a goal: it passes the scoring check. It is then stopped by the geometry-check defect (5.4) and by missing coach wording.
- Building team scoring by crossing a line, while the pressing team scores in a zone: with the ends exchanged, it passes every check and stops at missing coach wording. With the ends kept, it is refused, because the markings at each end would have to change between blocks. Today, a different target for each role passes the engine's checks only when the ends are exchanged.
- Winning the ball as a team's own score: refused. The engine's list of scoring events it can evaluate has no entry for winning the ball or for denial [knowledge: our draft, needs your ruling]. It is still open whether code is also needed. A score for winning the ball must also cite your relationship for its target, and our check could not yet make that pass. Winning the ball inside a zone already counts as an additional score, as in the zone version.
- The attacking (possession) team attacking a goal, as in finishing: refused. Only the pressing role can attack a goal [engine]. In Directional Possession Games, one of your rulings lets a version replace only the objective of the team at the far end [knowledge].
- Transition Games: refused, because it is only partly engine-readable [knowledge]. On it, a scoring bonus may not stand beside a team's own scoring event until you rule [engine: held for your ruling].
- Roles named "attacking" and "defending": refused. The engine accepts only "building" and "pressing", so every goal, including Stay Organized, is told "building team" and "pressing team" [engine].
- Scoring only inside a build-out episode cannot be reused. The engine has one kind of episode, the build-out, and it begins as Goalkeeper Build-Out defines it [knowledge: our draft, needs your ruling].
  What works: each team can have its own scoring event and target, and the check is general.
  What doesn't: only the pressing team can attack a goal; winning the ball cannot yet be a team's own score; only build-out episodes exist; the roles are always "building" and "pressing"; and each new combination lacks coach wording.
  Why it matters: finishing, pressing and regaining, the uses you named, are exactly the ones it does not yet reach.
  Blocks a pilot? Yes, for goals beyond Play Out from the Back.
  Decision needed: how winning the ball and denial should count as a team's score, and in which game forms. Also, whether role names may follow the goal (for example "attacking team" and "defending team").

5.2 Changing ends between blocks
Tested on: Stay Organized (zone version with the ends changed; goal version with the ends kept) and Secure Possession (a game without roles changing ends).
Result:
- WORKS for games with roles: served with every check passed, subject to caveat 1. A goal version with the ends kept is refused, correctly.
- FAILS for games without roles: teams can change ends only when they also exchange roles [engine].
- Team reassignment at a new block was not tested as a separate mechanism. The Stay Organized copies carried Play Out from the Back's goalkeeper reassignment and passed the block checks. In the copy with a goalkeeper on each team, the reassignment had to be removed to pass them.
  What works: changing ends at a new block, with its diagram and coach sentence, carries over to another goal.
  What doesn't: a game without roles cannot change ends between blocks.
  Why it matters: possession goals such as Secure Possession would need this if played in blocks.
  Blocks a pilot? No.
  Decision needed: none, unless your knowledge for such a goal calls for changing ends.

5.3 Versions chosen from the equipment available
Tested on: Stay Organized (goal version, then zone version, across six sessions), Progress the Attack (mini-goal version), and Stay Organized (full-size goal version).
Result: PARTIAL.
- Choosing a version and falling back work when the equipment is the only problem. The zone version is used with no goal, an unsuitable goal (such as a mini-goal where an FA goal is needed), one mini-goal where two are needed, or a full-size goal that does not fit 40 x 30 m. If goals are not stated, the app asks.
- The geometry-check defect (5.4) refused the preferred goal versions tried. By design, a refusal at that stage does not fall back.
- The defect also breaks the fallback at 0 goalkeepers and at 10 players. The goal version's refusal is left unclassified (its reason still names the shortfall), so the zone version is never tried. Play Out from the Back is not affected: at 0 goalkeepers it falls through correctly.
  What works: choosing a version by the equipment available is general.
  What doesn't: the geometry-check defect refused the goal versions tried (5.4) and, at 0 goalkeepers or 10 players, also stops the fallback.
  Why it matters: any goal that needs a physical goal, such as finishing, depends on fixing it.
  Blocks a pilot? No for the field test. Yes for an application pilot that includes any goal needing a physical goal.
  Decision needed: none; it is an engine defect.

5.4 Goals as targets, and penalty areas
Tested on: Stay Organized (a goalkeeper-defended goal with its penalty area; a goal without its penalty area; both teams with a goalkeeper; and a control copy that kept Play Out from the Back's starting positions relative to the marked line).
Result: FAILS, except in the control.
- The penalty-area rule is general: a defended goal without its penalty area is refused, correctly.
- One check confirms that positions measured from an end line still hold once the area is marked out. But once the area is marked out, the check skips the goal and the penalty area. It finds nothing left to measure and refuses the activity [engine: a defect, confirmed in the code by the independent check].
- The control copy passed this check, because its starting positions are stated relative to the marked line, and stopped at coach wording. Play Out from the Back's goal version passes for the same reason, so the position of its goal is never re-checked after the area is marked out; the penalty area is checked separately.
- A goal defended with a goalkeeper on each team cannot be represented [knowledge: our draft, needs your ruling].
- Sentences about a goal are withheld outside Play Out from the Back's configuration [engine].
  What works: the penalty-area relationship and its general check carry over.
  What doesn't: the check defect refuses every goal version tried whose positions are all measured from an end line. Games with a goalkeeper on each team cannot be represented. Goal sentences exist only for Play Out from the Back.
  Why it matters: the goal mechanism itself is held back by engine gaps (the check defect, goal wording) and by one draft entry of ours (one goalkeeper per defended goal). Finishing also needs its soccer knowledge: Finishing Games, the Finishing context and a Game Problem.
  Blocks a pilot? No for the field test, because the goal version's penalty area is still checked. Yes for an application pilot that includes another goal with a physical goal.
  Decision needed: none for the defect. Whether a goalkeeper on each team should be supported is a question for your finishing knowledge.

5.5 Required participants and goalkeeper relationships
Tested on: Stay Organized (the goalkeeper on the wrong team; a goalkeeper on both teams) and Secure Possession (restarts by any player, with no goalkeeper).
Result: WORKS, with one communication gap.
- A goalkeeper on the wrong team is refused with a clear coach sentence.
- Restarts with no goalkeeper are accepted.
- With a goalkeeper on both teams the activity is served, but the overview and diagram leave the goalkeepers out, the colour sentence stays singular, and the pressing team's goalkeeper is given no stated job [engine].
  What works: the check that a version has the participants it needs is general, and its refusals carry clear coach sentences.
  What doesn't: games with two goalkeepers are described incorrectly.
  Why it matters: it is minor, but it affects finishing games and games with two goals.
  Blocks a pilot? No.
  Decision needed: none.

5.6 One common restart, and the normal Laws by default
Tested on: Stay Organized (Play Out from the Back's restart rule; the normal Laws with a goalkeeper but no penalty area) and Secure Possession (restarts to the team that did not touch the ball last; restarts to the conceding team).
Result: PARTIAL.
- The inherited goalkeeper restart is served, with correct wording (subject to caveat 1).
- The normal-Laws account is general. It correctly reports that the goalkeeper's handling needs an adaptation when there is no penalty area.
- The game form's own restarts are described, as in a plain Directional Possession game. A restart the version itself assigns is a different case. For example, a restart to the team that did not touch the ball last passes every check but has no coach wording, so it is refused [engine].
- Restarts to the conceding team: a list of team descriptions that a version may not use ("attacking team", "defending team", "build-out team", "conceding team") applies to every goal. Any one of them stops the whole knowledge file of versions from loading. The list is ours, put to you on 17 October [knowledge: our draft, needs your ruling].
  What works: the one-restart rule and the normal-Laws account are general.
  What doesn't: restart sentences exist for the game form's own restarts and for the building team's goalkeeper, but not for other restarts a version assigns. Restarts to the conceding team are refused for every goal.
  Why it matters: most other activities restart without a goalkeeper.
  Blocks a pilot? No for Play Out from the Back. Yes for other goals.
  Decision needed: the same as decision 5 below (whether role names may follow the goal, which this list now forbids for every goal).

5.7 Coach communication (overview, details, diagram, team colours)
