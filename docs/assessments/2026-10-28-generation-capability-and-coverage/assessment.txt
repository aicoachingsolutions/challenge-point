GENERATION CAPABILITY AND LEARNING GOAL COVERAGE ASSESSMENT
In reply to your message of 28 October, Parts 3 to 5

This assessment proposes no new mechanisms. We ran the built application (database and AI simulated): 34 Play Out from the Back sessions and 304 across all 13 goals, before your Part 1 refinements (they change no count here), checked by a second, independent run. Detail: the appendix.

Terms
- Checked pathway: the knowledge-driven engine, building only from authored knowledge and running every structural check.
- AI-written pathway (your "legacy" one): code picks a game form, library constraints and scoring; an AI model (GPT-4o) writes the activity, checked only for format and basic mechanics.
- Engine-readable: knowledge restated so the engine can build from it.
- Authorization: your approval of what applies to one goal (context, Game Problem, constraints, versions).
- Coach wording: the engine's sentences for each part of a game, approved by you or drafted for your review; anything lacking one is refused.
- Two-to-four rule: a goal using any library constraint must use two to four, in a required mix of constraint roles.
- Geometry-check defect: an engine fault that refuses goal versions whose starting positions are all measured from an end line.

7. FINDINGS

Finding 1: Play Out from the Back is one activity, two versions
  What works: complete, checked, authorized; the version follows the equipment.
  What doesn't: no selectable constraint, incentive or structural variation.
  Why it matters: one authored activity is served, not generated.
  Blocks a pilot? No for the field test; yes for an application pilot.
  Decision needed: widen this goal first, or unlock others?

Finding 2: it reaches coaches through the live app
  What works: served exactly as reviewed.
  What doesn't: only within section 3's limits.
  Why it matters: what is built is what coaches get.
  Blocks a pilot? No.
  Decision needed: none.

Finding 3: ten goals reach coaches as AI-written, unchecked activities
  What works: with a simulated AI answer, the live app produced readable activities.
  What doesn't: unevaluated and unauthorized. When measured, nothing told the coach so, which did not meet your 26 October condition (item 9(a)) that their "status and limitations are not misrepresented"; we have since added a "Not checked" notice (draft wording) to every AI-written activity. Some scoring the checked engine refuses; A04, A05 and A06 lack a Game Problem.
  Why it matters: coaches could judge Challenge Point by activities never checked against your acceptance test.
  Blocks a pilot? Yes, if pilot coaches use them.
  Decision needed: is the notice enough for the field test and the pilot, or should these activities be withheld until checked?

Finding 4: coach inputs are mostly disconnected
  What works: for Play Out from the Back, equipment, players, goalkeepers and duration matter, and the coach is told which do not.
  What doesn't: Practice Situation, Learning Stage, Challenge Level (not asked in guided planning) and note shape no checked activity; the AI-written pathway passes only the Learning Stage to the AI, without telling the coach.
  Why it matters: your Part 4 requirement is unmet on both pathways.
  Blocks a pilot? Yes for testing how the app responds; not the field test.
  Decision needed: what each input should change, starting with the four Practice Situations.

Finding 5: five of the seven recent mechanisms are general in logic, but none gives another goal a valid activity yet
  What works: tested on other goals, the logic of per-team scoring, ends changing with roles, versions by equipment, required participants, and restarts under the normal Laws carried over.
  What doesn't: none of the seven yet gives a valid activity for another goal: the copies omitted each goal's context, and those that departed from Play Out from the Back's configuration stopped at coach wording.
  Why it matters: finishing, pressing and regaining are not yet reached.
  Blocks a pilot? Yes, beyond Play Out from the Back.
  Decision needed: section 6, items 5 and 6.
